Survey of 2026-11-13, one field, no experiments. Every source in the table in section 2 was opened today; quotes are from the opened text (PDF text extracted locally where the web reader could not parse it). Grades: **theorem** (proved in the source), **experiment** (measured in the source), **heuristic claim** (asserted or argued in the source). My own reasoning is marked **argued** and never put in the "source" column. ROUTES rows are cited by their route name in `2026-11-13/ROUTES.md`.

Bottom line first: this field contains a theorem that covers the measured E2 tree almost exactly, and it is a barrier, not a method. A backtracking tree with a single planted leaf whose subtrees are homogeneous (the "balanced" model) has a uniform runtime distribution; no restart strategy changes its exponent (Chen, Gomes, Selman 2001, Theorem 2). For every particle or importance-sampling method the matching result is that the sample size needed is about exp(KL divergence from the target to the proposal) (Chatterjee, Diaconis 2018, Theorem 1.1). For a single planted solution that is 1/(the probability the proposal puts on it). Splitting, SMC, population annealing and the cross-entropy method all fall under that bound. They gain only the bits of information their weights carry about which prefix is planted. The repo's measurements say that this information is about 20 bits, all at the corners.

## 1. The analog

**Rare-event simulation.** The event "a random root-to-leaf path of the row-scan tree is the planted solution" has probability 2^-134 or less under any uninformed proposal. Multilevel splitting, sequential Monte Carlo (SMC) and subset simulation estimate such probabilities by moving a population through a sequence of nested levels. In E2 the levels are depths (cells filled), the population is a set of valid partial boards, and the "importance function" is the depth. Track 10's tree estimator is exactly this object: an SMC with N particles, resampled by Knuth weight at each depth.

**Backtracking runtime theory.** Chen, Gomes and Selman model backtracking as a tree with "exactly a single successful leaf". Their balanced model (homogeneous subtrees, no propagation) and imbalanced model (wrong branches lead to subtrees of very different sizes) are the two poles. E2 with five clues is the single-leaf case: E[Z] = 4.1e-8 for a random instance with these colour counts, so the planted solution is essentially the only one (ROUTES, first-moment solution counts).

**Planted inference for Monte Carlo methods.** In planted clique, planted spin glasses and tile-planted Ising models, simulated annealing (SA), parallel tempering (PT) and population annealing (PA) look for a hidden configuration by following an energy. In E2 the energy is the number of mismatched edges, and the hidden configuration is the planted tiling.

**Where the analogy breaks.**
- *All-different.* Splitting and SMC theory assumes a Markov process whose state at level k carries everything needed for the future. A partial E2 board does carry the used-tile set, so the Markov property holds, but the state space is the tree itself. No compressed "reaction coordinate" exists unless something carries information about the planted prefix.
- *Energy is not a funnel.* Planted clique and planted spin glasses have an energy that correlates with overlap with the plant. In E2, boards at 470 sit 246 to 254 cells from a solution in expectation (ROUTES, "A 470 board as a damaged solution", argued, not refereed). Energy-driven samplers (SA, PT, PA, CE) therefore target the wrong set.
- *Fixed size.* Every theorem below is asymptotic, in n, the depth or a rarity parameter. E2 is one instance at n = 16. The theorems are used here as statements about mechanism, and the arithmetic in section 4 is done at the real size.

## 3. Hardness evidence

**B1. Restarts cannot change the exponent on a balanced single-leaf tree** (Chen, Gomes, Selman 2001, Lemma 2 and Theorem 2, theorem). *Assumptions:* a complete b-ary tree of depth n, exactly one successful leaf, chronological backtracking, random child order, no pruning. The single-leaf case is the paper's setting; footnote 2 says multiple solutions do "not qualitatively change our results". *Does it hold for E2?* The row-scan tree is not complete: it dies off and has a peak at free depth 158. The proof needs one thing, that a run which has explored t nodes holds the planted leaf with probability about t/T. That holds whenever the subtrees hanging off the planted path are about the size their depth predicts. **Argued:** Track 10's measurements are evidence for that homogeneity. The first-moment model, which assumes every partial board at depth k behaves like an average one, predicts the total to 0.03 dex at three clue settings (Real 16x16 tree estimator row). The SMC's 95% interval at N = 1e6 is ±0.017 dex, which is a per-particle relative variance of about 940 (2^9.9; my arithmetic from the interval, 5 reps). That is moderate dispersion of subtree weight, not an exponentially imbalanced tree. The official set also sits in the middle of 20 boards painted from its colour census (5 of 20 below it). Under these conditions Luby's l(t) = t/q(t) with q(t) ≈ t/T gives l ≈ T against a plain-DFS mean of T/2. So a restart portfolio **loses about 1 bit** on the plain row-scan tree, and no cutoff schedule gains an exponent.

**B2. Heavy tails need imbalance, and imbalance fades toward criticality** (Gomes et al. 2005, experiment; Chen et al. 2001, theorem for the model). *Assumptions:* random binary CSP, not edge matching; the threshold moves with the solver. *For E2:* the instance was built at the density where E[Z] is about 1 (first-moment solution counts row). This is the "homogeneously long" regime of the 2005 paper. The one escape the papers name is a propagating solver whose refutations are short on some branches and long on others: "effective branching heuristics with propagation mechanisms". That is untested here for bb-mrv. The ROUTES row on propagating DFS says the lead of bb-mrv over plain DFS does not grow with n at 6x6 to 8x8, which counts weakly against an exploitable imbalance.

**B3. Any particle or importance-sampling search needs about 1/mu(solution) samples** (Chatterjee, Diaconis 2018, Theorem 1.1, theorem). *Assumptions:* estimating an expectation under nu by sampling from mu, with log rho concentrated. **Argued:** for search, take nu to be the point mass on the planted path. Then L = D(nu‖mu) = -ln mu(planted path) exactly, with no fluctuation (s = 0), and the theorem says that fewer than exp(L) samples almost surely miss the plant. Splitting, SMC, PA, subset simulation and CE are all ways of building mu adaptively, so all of them pay 2^(-log2 mu(plant)). Reweighting can lower that exponent only by putting more mass on the planted prefix than on its siblings, which takes a statistic correlated with being planted. In this repo the measured supply of such information is about 20 bits, all at the four corners (17.45 exact), plus about 2 ranking bits (ROUTES, cheap information about the frame, refereed).

**B4. Splitting is efficient only when its levels follow the most likely path to the rare set** (Glasserman et al. 1998, theorem, necessary conditions). In E2 the only level function available without solution information is depth or score. The most likely path to "the planted board" is the planted path itself, which no depth-indexed level set distinguishes from its siblings. **Argued:** with depth as the level, an SMC with N particles holds the planted prefix at depth k with probability about N/W_k. Section 4 does the arithmetic.

**B5. Energy-driven samplers fail on planted structure in the theorem cases** (Chen, Mossel, Zadik 2022, theorem; Coquelin, Munos 2007, theorem). *Assumptions:* planted clique in G(n,1/2), which is not E2. The Metropolis process and simulated tempering fail for every polynomial clique size below linear. UCT's worst-case regret is doubly exponential in depth. *Counter-evidence:* Angelini 2018 finds PT polynomial in the hard region of planted clique at the sizes tested (experiment). The difference is that planted clique has a funnel: density rises toward the clique. **Argued:** E2 has no funnel to the plant, since high-score boards are 246 to 254 cells from a solution in expectation (argued, not refereed in ROUTES). So the E2 case is the pessimistic side of these results, and the A10 overlap-gap kill on 5x5 does not rescue it (ROUTES flags it as non-transferable).

**What the 0.03-digit agreement implies.** **Argued**, combining B1 to B4.
1. *Heavy tails.* The first-moment model is a statement about means at each depth. Matching the measured total to 0.03 dex, with an SMC per-particle variance near 2^10 and an ordinary instance, puts the plain row-scan tree in the balanced regime of B1. Chen et al.'s imbalanced model needs subtree sizes that vary exponentially with depth of error (p > 1/b^2). If that variation existed at the depths that hold the mass (free depths 120 to 175), Knuth weights would have heavy tails and N = 1e6 particles could not have produced a ±0.017 dex interval. Caveat: SMC resampling hides some of the tail, and extinction at free depths 188 to 192 leaves the deep tree unobserved. The claim therefore covers the bulk where 99% of the nodes sit (sum to free depth 175 = 10^40.453), not the tail.
2. *Reweighting.* If the model were badly wrong, a reweighting by the true conditional subtree size would be worth the gap between model and truth. A gap of 0.03 dex in the total leaves almost nothing to recover by reweighting with tree-size information. The planted solution changes the count by a factor of 1 + E[Z], and that factor is invisible in T. Any further gain must come from information about the planted prefix specifically (B3), not from estimating the tree better.
3. *Extinction.* Track 10's population collapses onto about 300 effective families by free depth 175 and dies at 188 to 192, where the level width is still about 10^35. This is the in-house demonstration of B4: depth-indexed splitting with 10^6 particles keeps 10^-33 of the level.

## 4. Transfer candidates

Baseline: 2^134.4 nodes (10^40.457). Peak level width 10^39.16 = 2^130.1 (Track 10, measured). Arithmetic is in base 2 unless stated.

**Not a candidate: splitting, SMC or PA as a finding method.** Depth-level splitting with N particles holds the planted prefix through the peak with probability about N/W_peak. **Argued**, assuming resampling is near uniform over the level, which is what an unbiased Knuth-weight SMC targets. One run needs N ≈ W_peak = 2^130.1 particles, each pushed through about 256 depths: 2^130.1 × 2^8 = **2^138.1 nodes**, 3.7 bits above the baseline. With N = 1e6, a run succeeds with probability 10^(6 - 39.16) = 10^-33.2. Repeating costs 10^33.2 × 10^6 × 256 = 10^41.6 = **2^138.1**, the same. Reweighting by score (the record engines' energy) points away from the plant (B5). CE and NRPA learn a product or rollout policy from elite samples, and their elites are the 460 to 470 class, which carries no information about the plant's location. No transfer.

**C1. Measure the runtime distribution of randomised backtracking on the critical ladder, plain and propagating.**
- *Statement:* on planted near-critical boards, the survival function of randomised row-scan DFS (random value order) is not heavy-tailed, and the best fixed cutoff gives l*/E[T] >= 0.5 at every size. For bb-mrv with random tie-breaking, l*/E[T] does not fall with n.
- *Cost on E2:* a gain needs q(t) >> t/T for some small t. In bits the gain is log2(E[T]/l*). Under B1 that is -1 bit for plain DFS, so the result is **135.4 bits** (2^134.4 × 2, guess-free under the balanced model). For a propagating solver the gain is unknown. Changing the exponent would need l*/E[T] to shrink geometrically with n. To reach 1,000 core-years (about 17 to 20 orders, or 56 to 66 bits) the ratio would have to fall by about a factor of 2 per added row of the board. That rate is a guess.
- *Cheapest kill:* the existing critical rungs (6x6 c=4 to 8x8, 30 instances each), 200 randomised runs per instance and solver, to completion. Compute the empirical q(t), l* = min_t l(t), and the log-log survival slope. A few core-hours on one machine. Kill if l*/median(T) >= 0.5 at all three sizes for both solvers, or if the ratio does not fall from 6x6 to 8x8. Track 5's `fc-luby` seeding bug must be fixed first, or the runs are not independent.
- *Odds:* plain DFS changes its exponent, under 1%. bb-mrv shows a ratio falling with n, about 5%. That it matters for a full solve, under 0.5%. Worth running only because it is cheap and would turn the EXPLORED "killed (reported)" restart row into a measurement.

**C2. Extreme pruning with re-randomisation: cash in the corner bits at success probability p.**
- *Statement:* prune with a test that the planted prefix passes with probability p and a uniform prefix passes with probability q < p. Repeat over independent re-randomisations (orientation of the clue frame, choice of corner, scan direction). Total cost is T q/p, a gain of log2(p/q) bits.
- *Cost:* the measured information budget is about 17.45 bits at the corners plus about 2 ranking bits. ROUTES says part of the corner information is already inside the row-scan price ("true for two corners"), so the uncashed part is a guess of about 8 to 10 bits. Best case 134.4 - 10 = **124.4 bits**, still 2^58 or more above 1,000 core-years. Extreme pruning adds nothing beyond that budget. In GNR's analogy the gain comes from the treasure being behind door 1 with probability 25%, and here the doors are uniform (B3). The re-randomisations are also not independent here: there is one instance and one plant, while lattices offer many bases.
- *Cheapest kill:* on 20 painted 8x8 planted boards, for the corner-block test and the colour-ledger chi-square test (path 4), measure the pass rate of planted prefixes against all valid prefixes at the same depth. The gain is log2(p_planted/p_all). One core-hour. Kill if the summed gain at 8x8 is under 3 bits, or if it is already recovered by row scan's own dead ends.
- *Odds:* gains more than 10 bits on the real board, about 3%. Closes the gap, essentially 0.

**C3. Use the validated SMC estimator to rank static fill orders on the real tiles before anything is built.**
- *Statement:* Track 10's estimator (32 s per repetition at N = 1e6) can price any static order on the real 16x16 with five clues in minutes. The ROUTES row on other fill orders says the "killed" verdict rests on uncommitted small-board data, and the planted-jigsaw literature says order can change the exponent. This is Kilby et al.'s and Belov et al.'s use of estimators (choosing the algorithm on the instance), applied at full size.
- *Cost:* the gain in bits is log2(T_row/T_order) and is unknown. The growth-law memo predicts surface terms of order n ln n only, a guess of 0 to 10 bits. Frame-first is already priced at 10^45.9 (worse). Result: 134.4 - g, with g between 0 and 10 bits as a guess.
- *Cheapest kill:* re-run Track 10's 6x6 gate for each new order (the estimator is validated only for row scan), then 5 repetitions at N = 1e6 for column scan, the four rotations of row scan (the clues break the symmetry), spiral-in, diagonal and clue-corridor orders. Under one core-hour. Kill if every order lands within 1 dex of 40.457.
- *Odds:* some order beats row scan by more than 1 dex, about 15%. By more than 17 dex, under 0.1%. Even a negative result is worth having: it replaces an EXPLORED row graded "claim without committed data" with a 16x16 measurement.

**Three papers to read in full:**
1. Chen, Gomes, Selman 2001 (with the long version's multiple-solution case): it is the formal model that C1 tests, and its balanced/imbalanced dichotomy is the cleanest statement of why restarts cannot help here.
2. Chatterjee, Diaconis 2018, sections 1 and 3 (Knuth-type path examples): it gives the sample-size lower bound that covers every particle method, and an estimator of D(nu‖mu) that could be computed from Track 10's raw weights.
3. Gomes, Fernández, Selman, Bessière 2005: it measured where heavy tails vanish relative to the transition and how propagation shifts that point. That is the design template for C1 and the place to check whether a propagating solver at critical density is expected to be balanced.
