Nothing here is Eternity II progress. Grades: measured (a script in `scripts/geometry-sdp-lift/` produced it), derived, guessed.

**5x5 (measured, three boards): the lift is not closed at 5x5.** Both conditions pass on all three boards.
- z against annealed near-solutions: 2.60 and 3.42 on the two boards whose solve converged (seeds 1 and 2), and 2.71 on seed 0, whose solve did not converge (residuals 2.4e-5 and 5.0e-5 after 8,200 iterations; its point is close to the converged picture but is not a reading by the standard I set).
- Placements the SDP point puts below 1e-4 while arc consistency keeps them: 325, 268 and 173 (seed 0 unconverged). On seed 1 all 325 are certified dead by a second solve (no feasible point of the lift puts more than 4e-5 in total on them).

What actually happens, and why the z of about 3 understates it: on the two converged 5x5 boards the SDP is tight in x. Its point lies in the convex hull of the true solutions (hull distance 0.0000), puts no mass (below 1e-5 per cell) on any placement that no solution uses, and Z has numerical rank 6 and 7 against a solution span of rank 6 and 8. The lift, solved once, already knows which placements belong to a solution: 93 of the 418 placements arc consistency keeps on seed 1, 106 of 373 on seed 2. Near-solutions are "separated" because, on average, 11 to 14 of their 25 cells sit on placements the lift has killed; z is only 2.6 to 3.4 because those kills make the near-solutions' own bits very spread (sd 48 to 64 bits).

None of the three baselines comes close on the same boards. The LP (same constraints without PSD) gives z of 0.17 to 1.02 against annealed controls, arc consistency 0.33 to 0.42, probe B's flow -0.26 to 0.42. At 6x6 the baselines ran on all three boards and stay below 2 everywhere (largest: flow 1.26 on seed 0).

The cost is what kills the idea as a solver, not the signal: a 25-cell toy that row-scan DFS enumerates in about 2^15 nodes (well under a second) costs the SDP 400 to 1,100 seconds, and the 36-cell toy (2^21.8 nodes) did not finish in an hour. On the 16x16 the matrix has about 2^34.5 entries (derived in far-ideas-geometry.md); a positive at toy scale changes nothing there without a decomposition nobody has.

## 1. What was built

**Boards.** Generated 5x5 (cf 3, ci 4) seeds 0, 1, 2 and 6x6 (cf 4, ci 5) seeds 0, 1, 2 (seeds 3 and 4 added on x86), every solution enumerated by probe B's row-scan DFS. Pin: the corner tile with the smallest index, at cell 0 (top-left), in its only rotation with grey on N and W; that tile is excluded from every other cell (probe B's convention, `pin_of` in `common.py`). Tile ids per board are in each table header.

**Variables and constraints** (`lift.py`). x[p] for each rim-consistent placement p = (cell, tile, rotation): 478 at 5x5, 1,290 at 6x6. Z = [1 x^T; x Y] is PSD, Y[p,p] = x[p]. Each cell and each tile sums to 1. Row sums: for every p and every other cell c, the sum of Y[p,q] over q in c equals x[p], and the same for every other tile. Y[p,q] is the constant 0 (not a variable) when p and q share a cell, share a tile, or sit in adjacent cells with mismatched facing sides. x and Y are entrywise nonnegative. The LP is the same system without the PSD cone. Free off-diagonal pairs: about 86,500 at 5x5 and 676,639 at 6x6 seed 0.

**Objective: minimise ||x||^2.** It is the same fixed function on every board, symmetric under any relabelling of cells, tiles and rotations, blind to colours and to the solutions (matching enters only through the constraints), and strictly convex, so x is unique. An entropy-like spread was the first plan; I tried maximising sum of -x log x first. On the 4x4 check SCS never met its dual tolerance in 200,000 iterations (the optimum has most x at 0, where the entropy's slope is infinite), while ||x||^2 converged in about 175 iterations to the same x (`speedtest_entropy_vs_l2.py`, `raw/speedtest_entropy_vs_l2.out`). Minimising the trace would not work: trace(Y) = sum of x = number of cells on the whole feasible set. The price of ||x||^2: unlike the entropy, its optimum need not give every feasible placement positive mass. So measure (c) never reads a zero off this point alone. A second solve maximises the sum of x over the candidate zeros; an optimum of 0 means every feasible point has them all at 0 ("certified" below). The caveat is real: on 5x5 seed 2 the point gives about 0 to one placement that a solution uses.

**Code check (measured, `validate.py`, `raw/validate_4x4.json`).** On a 4x4 board (cf 2, ci 4, seed 3) with exactly one solution under the pin, the solution's rank-one lift satisfies every constraint of both relaxations exactly (equality residual 0, smallest entry 0, smallest eigenvalue of Z -4e-15), and a random rim-consistent board violates them (residual 1, smallest eigenvalue -1.7). Solved, the SDP returns exactly that solution (x within 5e-10, Z of rank one, rounding gives the solution); the LP does not (its point is 0.92 from it). The same check ran on x86 before any 6x6 solve, with the same result. Every run also checks that every enumerated solution's rank-one lift is feasible before solving (`all_solutions_rank_one_feasible` in each result: residual 0 on all boards).

**Near-solution controls** (`controls_c.py`, `nearctl.c`). Probe B's two definitions, unchanged: complete boards with at most 2 mismatched inner edges and at least 8 cells from every solution, found by probe B's annealer ("annealed") or by its mismatch-tolerant randomised DFS ("sampled"), 20 of each per board. Probe B found none at 6x6; I ported both generators to C with the same moves, schedule and filters (only the RNG differs) and checked the port on 5x5: 20 kept out of 516 anneals of 15,000 steps on seed 1, against probe B's 7 of 150. At 6x6 the anneals need 5 million steps each (the Python annealer at 1 million steps reached 3 mismatches at best, `raw/python_annealer_reach_6_4_5_0.json`), and the DFS 300 to 620 million nodes for 20 boards. Every kept board is rechecked in Python. The 200 random rim-consistent boards are probe B's.

**Measures** (`measure.py`). (b) bits on a board s: the sum over cells of log2(x[c, s_c] / X0[c, s_c]), X0 probe B's centre, x floored at 1e-5 (the solver tolerance); z = (mean over solutions minus mean over controls) / sd over controls, as in probe B. (a) hull distance: the smallest max-norm distance from x to a convex combination of the solutions (LP); "mass off solutions": the x mass per cell on placements no solution uses; numerical rank of Z (eigenvalues above 1e-3 of the largest) against the rank of the solutions' span; rounding: probe B's Hungarian rounding on x, and on Z's leading eigenvector. (c) placements with x below 1e-4 that arc consistency keeps, and the reverse. Arc consistency is plain AC to a fixpoint on cells, with matching and different-tile constraints between neighbours and different-tile constraints between all cells, nothing else. The AC baseline point is the Sinkhorn balancing of the AC domains (the Birkhoff centre restricted to them). The flow baseline is probe B's soft point at the temperature where solutions beat random boards most, median of 4 runs.

## 4. What this does and does not say

Measured: on three 5x5 toys and one 4x4 toy, the degree-2 lift with PSD, gangster and mismatch zeros, nonnegativity and row sums is tight or nearly tight in x. One solve gives the set of placements that belong to some solution, and that set is 3 to 4 times smaller than what arc consistency keeps. Without the PSD cone the same constraints are nowhere near (LP hull distance 0.17 to 0.37, and z against annealed controls at most 1.02). So the gain is the PSD condition itself, not the second-order linear constraints, which agrees with gap 6a finding nothing in degree-2 linear algebra. Hungarian rounding of x never gives a solution, because the point is a mixture of several solutions. With the support known, a search restricted to it is trivial at this size.

Not measured, and not to be inferred: whether tightness survives at 6x6. Small instances with few solutions are where level-2 lifts are most often exact (guessed, from the general behaviour of such hierarchies, not checked here). The 6x6 runs are the test.

Derived, and unchanged by any toy result: the cost. The SDP took 400 to 1,100 seconds on a board DFS clears in under a second, and the 6x6 factorisation alone has run for over 30 minutes at 6.5 GB. The 16x16 lift has about 2^17.3 placements and 2^34.5 matrix entries. A toy-scale signal bears on Eternity II only through a decomposition (local lifts glued by consistency) that this experiment does not touch. My odds for 10 bits on the real puzzle stay below 0.5 percent until the 6x6 point and a decomposition sketch exist.
