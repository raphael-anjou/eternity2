## 0. The finding that reframes the question

The S6 tilt is what the program's own random model predicts, and its size is predicted too. In a planted ensemble the true prefix is distributed as a uniform consistent prefix reweighted by its number of completions. S6 is the first-moment log of that number given the census. For a log-weight that is roughly normal with sd sigma (nats) over the population, the reweighted mean moves by sigma^2, so delta = sigma. Calibration rows (10 boards, 40 rows per depth):

| depth | sd of S6 over the sample (nats) | S6(true) minus mean | shift / sd^2 | delta | S9 delta | corr of S6 and S9 true-prefix deltas |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 96 | 0.90 | +0.98 | 1.22 | +1.10 | -0.95 | -0.86 |
| 128 | 1.08 | +1.53 | 1.30 | +1.43 | -1.20 | -0.90 |
| 144 | 1.17 | +1.80 | 1.32 | +1.57 | -1.38 | -0.92 |
| 160 | 1.26 | +2.48 | 1.56 | +2.00 | -1.66 | -0.87 |
| 176 | 1.37 | +2.85 | 1.51 | +2.12 | -1.68 | -0.91 |

Pure size bias predicts a ratio of 1; measured 1.2 to 1.6 (the pilot's 6 boards: 2.0 to 2.2, driven by board 7002 at delta 5 to 7.8). So: (a) S6 and S9 are one statistic, not two (correlation -0.9; S4 is a third reading of the same census); (b) a tilt of about sigma is not a contradiction of "the tree's level sizes match the random model", it is a consequence of it; (c) the excess over 1 and the heavy board-to-board spread (per-board sd about 1, one board in 16 above 5) are the part not explained here. The README must say (b) in its first paragraph if the test confirms.

Second point: the peak depths are where a prune is worth least. With the calibration mean widths (log10: 34.90 at 96, 38.27 at 128, 38.87 at 144, 38.98 at 160, 37.75 at 176; log-linear interpolation between target depths, total 10^40.41), the share of the tree's nodes strictly below depth d is 0.97 at 128, 0.71 at 144, 0.18 at 160, 0.004 at 176. One prune at depth d cannot gain more than log2(total / nodes at or above d): 4.9 bits at 128, 1.8 at 144, 0.3 at 160, 0.01 at 176. The decision depths of Reading 1 measure the tilt where it cannot be cashed. The interpolation is crude; the study must redo it from every level (change 1).

## 1. Changes that MUST be made before the test run (amendment A4)

1. **Record the level profile and the family diagnostic.** Per (board, direction): write `est` (log10 width) at every level. Set `ANC_DEPTHS = [96, 112, 128, 144]` and `DIAG_DEPTHS = [128, 144, 160, 176]` and write, per row at depth k, the number of distinct ancestors at depth k - 32 and the family effective size 1 / sum(share^2) of the whole population (not of the m sampled). A row is invalid for every decision if family effective size < 200 or population < 1,000. A depth with more than 10% invalid rows is demoted to descriptive for all statistics. Before the test run, rerun calibration board 7007 with the new binary and check every pre-existing field is byte-identical.
2. **Separate sound census pruning from the tilt.** Per row, record how many of the m sampled prefixes are census-dead (some colour with r_c < f_c) and assert the true prefix never is (stop with an error if it is). Compute the reference mean and sd of S4, S6, S9 over census-feasible sampled prefixes only. Report the dead fraction per depth as exact pruning, with its own line, never inside a delta.
3. **Add Reading 3 (survivors).** At depths 128, 144, 160: evaluate S10 (descendants 16 cells ahead, the existing node cap) on population members taken in a seeded random order that does not touch the SMC's RNG, until 1,000 with S10 > 0 are found or the population is exhausted. delta_surv = (s(true) - mean over those survivors) / sd over those survivors, for S4, S5, S6, S9. Fewer than 200 survivors: unmeasured. Also record the surviving fraction. A census statistic is called a usable tilt only if it passes Readings 1, 2 and 3 at the same depth. r = 2 (32 cells) is reported if at least 200 of the r = 1 survivors pass it under a 1e6 node cap, otherwise stated as unmeasured.
4. **Reading 2: raise the work ESS floor from 10 to 30** and print, beside every work delta, the median work ESS. State in the README that at 160 and 176 the work weights are close to uniform (median ESS 184 and 392 of 1,000 on calibration, because the tree no longer grows), so Reading 2 cannot differ from Reading 1 there; its informative depths are 128 and 144.
5. **Add the value reading, fixed now.** For S6 only, at depths 96, 128, 144, with A = nodes at levels <= d and B = nodes below (from change 1, per board and direction): capped gain G = log2(p (A + B) / (C (A + f B))), f the kept fraction of the work-weighted reference, p the share of rows whose true prefix is kept, taken from the empirical percentile table (not the normal formula), at the f that gives p >= 0.5 and again p >= 0.8. K = 1 (best direction, C = 1), K = 2 (the two cheapest directions) and K = 4 (C = summed direction totals over the best, measured per board), success = at least one direction keeps the true prefix on the same board. Bootstrap over boards, 95% interval. The word "usable" in any summary requires the lower end of G above 1 bit at some depth. A pass of Reading 1 at 160 or 176 alone is reported as "a tilt at a depth where it saves under 0.3 bits".
6. **Keep 96 boards, 30 tests, no split.** Add to the report, per statistic and peak depth: median, quartiles and the share of boards with per-board delta below 0.7 and below 0, since the real puzzle is one board. Declare S6 the primary census statistic and S4, S9 as readings of the same quantity (report their correlation with S6 on the true prefix); a pass of S6 and S9 together is one finding.
7. **Direction correlation by pair type.** Report the correlation of the true prefix's delta and of the kept-tail indicator separately for opposite directions (0 with 2, 1 with 3) and adjacent ones, at each of 96, 128, 144, 160, 176, not pooled over the peak only.
8. **Register the painter control now; it may run beside the test.** On 6x6 boards with exact enumeration, with the colour count lowered until boards have several solutions (fresh seeds 9101 on, at least 30 boards with 2 or more solutions): the exact delta of S4, S6, S9 on prefixes of the non-planted solutions against the planted one's. Pass (no painter signature): the planted mean lies inside the 95% interval of the non-planted mean at each depth. No claim that a census tilt transfers beyond planted boards may be written before this is in.

## 2. Rulings

**1. Delta and the comparison. Keep, with changes 1 and 2.** The sampler lists every child of the population and keeps N distinct ones uniformly (Floyd), no weights, no duplication, so within a run all particles weigh the same and the plain mean is the right one. Inclusion of a node is proportional to the product of N/e_j along its path; e_j is random, so the sample is uniform only up to that fluctuation, which G-exact bounds (bias at most 0.08 sd). The m = 1,000 are a uniform draw without replacement: fine. The reference sd is the right denominator for placing a threshold; it is not enough for a gain, because the true class is not unit-variance (per-board sd about 1 on a four-direction mean, one board at +5), hence the empirical table in change 5. Degeneracy: particles are clustered in families, and a census statistic is mostly inherited from the ancestor, so few families shrink the within-run sd and inflate delta. The gate files show it: at N = 1e4 (360 to 900 particles at 176) S6 and S9 deltas at 176 are 5 to 60% larger than at 1e5; from 1e5 to 1e6 the S6 delta at 176 still falls (7.6 to 7.2, 2.17 to 2.02), a residual inflation of about 5 to 7%, not enough to create a pass. Width estimates at 176 show no missing mass (1e5: 38.05 to 38.20 and 38.10 to 38.41; 1e6: 38.04 and 38.24). The diagnostic and threshold are change 1. I expect 144 and 160 to pass easily (the population is at or near the cap) and 176 to be the depth at risk.

**2. Artefact or property. Change before the test run (changes 2, 3, 8); one part cannot be fixed today.** (a) Census-dead prefixes are rare at 16x16 (the 1e6 gate run met one per board at 176), so the tilt is a shift among feasible prefixes, not disguised parity pruning; change 2 makes that a recorded number. (b) By section 0 the tilt is the size-bias identity; any painter that draws joint colours without spatial structure gives it. The generator has a count-preserving repair loop that swaps seam colours around offending tiles; I did not audit what triggers it. Change 8 tests for a signature exactly. Cannot be fixed today: whether the real solution's colours are spread like a uniform draw. A designer who balanced colours by region would shrink or reverse the tilt, and no data on the real solution exists to check it. The README must state the transfer as conditional on that. (c) Reading 2 is the right correction in principle and its estimator is acceptable at 128 and 144 with the higher floor (change 4); it is uninformative at 160 and 176. Reading 3 is needed and cheap (change 3): it is the direct form of "does the statistic know anything search does not learn within a row". My expectation, not a result: S6 survives it largely, because the census is a slow variable (work delta 1.40 against node delta 1.57 at 144).

**3. Negative control. Keep the decoy; it is not sufficient; add change 8.** The decoy catches any asymmetry between the replay path and the population path and any error in the reference. It cannot catch a property of planted prefixes, which is the only alternative explanation that matters here. Re-painting the remaining tiles with the same census is not a control for census statistics: S4, S6, S9 of the "true" prefix are unchanged by construction, so it would show the same delta with no solution behind it. That is worth one sentence in the README (a census tilt is a likelihood ratio, not a certificate) but not a run. Planting two solutions is not constructible with each tile used once. The buildable control is the non-planted solutions of small boards (change 8).

**4. Positive control at 176. Keep, and say what it is.** S10 and S7 saturate at +10 by the sd = 0 rule, so the control shows that the true prefix replays and has descendants where the population has none; it does not test the delta scale (S8 at +3.4 does, weakly). The population dying before 192 does not mean a different tree: the estimator is unbiased level by level and extinction barely moves with N (185 to 190 at 1e4, 188 to 193 at 1e5, 192 at 1e6), which says the depth-192 level hangs under a share of depth-160 nodes far below 1e-6. Plain search walks the same nodes. The consequence is the one in section 0: below 176 there is almost nothing left to save.

**5. Power and multiplicity. Keep; do not split.** The test boards are untouched and every definition is fixed, so the test is confirmatory whatever happened on calibration; A2 changed S6 before any test board and is disclosed. A split would cost power exactly where it is short: the true mean of S6 at 144 sits near the 1.4 line (calibration 1.57, se about 0.1 at 96 boards), so "mean >= 1.4" is itself close to a coin flip at 144 with 48 boards. The forking path that remains is in the reading, not the test: two flagged statistics that are one (change 6), and a pass at depths where it is worthless (change 5).

**6. Value. See section 3.** The dihedral presentations give nothing beyond the four directions: a quarter turn of the board is the next scan direction, and a reflection only reverses the order inside a row, so at row boundaries the prefix cells and the census are identical. Relabelling or rotating tiles leaves a census statistic unchanged. Other cell orders are different trees, 10 digits more expensive in the model. So K is at most 4, and 2 at equal cost.

**7. Arc consistency. A separate track; argument and measurement.** At 160 every sampled prefix is refuted, work-weighted too (work mean 0, sd 0, ESS 184), so all nodes below 160 hang under refutable nodes and search does pay for them: it is not "a few nodes later". But the saving is bounded by the share of the tree below the depth where refutation becomes general: about 71% below 144 and 18% below 160, so 0.3 to 1.8 bits in nodes if it starts between 144 and 160, more only if it bites by 128 (cap 4.9). Against that, S7 costs 0.77 ms per call here, about 2^13 plain nodes. The deciding measurement: the same SMC estimator on the real pieces with a child kept only if arc consistent, giving the total node count of the propagating tree, multiplied by the ns per node of an incremental propagator, against Track 10's total times its ns per node.

**8. A3. Keep; compromises nothing.** The stage is deterministic, uses no test seed, feeds only the descriptive "null in reverse", and one depth-32 line was seen. Check only that the rerun of exact-9001 and 9002 is committed before the gate reading that uses them.

## 3. Gain estimate (item 6)

Assumptions: normal statistic, unit variance in both classes (false for the true class, see ruling 1), one prune at one depth, kept and dropped nodes cost the same below (Reading 2), level profile interpolated from calibration means, direction costs from the second analysis (1, 2.02 for the two cheap ones, 2^4.19 for all four), equicorrelated directions, success on the one real instance required to be at least 0.5. Deltas are calibration values (10 boards), work-weighted where available.

| prune depth | delta | cap | K = 1 | K = 2, rho 0.23 | K = 4, rho 0.23 | K = 4, rho 0 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 96 | 1.10 (node) | 17.2 | +1.9 | +2.1 | +0.2 | +0.6 |
| 128 | 1.34 | 4.9 | +2.1 | +1.9 | -0.7 | -0.5 |
| 144 | 1.40 | 1.8 | +0.7 | +0.1 | -2.9 | -2.8 |
| 160 | 1.85 | 0.3 | +0.1 | -0.8 | -4.0 | -3.9 |
| 176 | 2.05 | 0.01 | 0.0 | -1.0 | -4.2 | -4.2 |

At success 0.8 the K = 1 figures are 1.0, 1.3, 0.7 bits. Without the cap (all work below the prune) one presentation at success 0.5 gives log2(0.5 / Q(delta)) = 2.6 bits at delta 1.4 and 4.8 at 2.1, and the calibration's empirical table agrees (keep 10%: 5.3 to 6 times, 2.4 to 2.6 bits). Sentence for the README, if the test confirms: "a census tilt of about 1.4 to 2 sd, if it holds on the real puzzle, is worth about 2 bits (one scan direction, pruned once near depth 96 to 128, half the time losing the solution); four directions lose 0.5 to 4 bits because they cost 2^4.19 and are correlated at about 0.23; at the widest levels the same tilt saves under 1 bit". Not estimated: pruning at several depths. The census is a slow variable, so successive prunes are strongly dependent and their gains do not add; that needs a sequential reading on the test rows (S6 of the true prefix at 96, 128, 144 jointly), which change 5 makes possible.

Against a gap of about 60 bits, 2 bits is a constant factor. A confirmed tilt would falsify O2 as worded ("no statistic points at the solution") and should be reported as that, with its price.
