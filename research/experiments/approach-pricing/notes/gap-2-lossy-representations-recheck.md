# Review of "Gap 2: lossy representations of the used set"

Second analysis report, 2026-11-13. Scripts and outputs in `notes/gap-2-review/`. The memo was not edited. Figures are from the first-moment model on the real colour counts with five clues (`m12_model.py` of the derivations second analysis), unless a line says toy. Toy boards are generated boards and say nothing about Eternity II beyond checking an argument.

## Verdict

The memo's conclusion survives, but three of the reasons given for it do not, and its headline number is wrong.

1. "Empty" stands for the family the memo names, with a narrower scope than it claims: lossy states that are functions of tile ids and frontier colours chosen without looking at the tile colours, one table, region lists. The largest gain I can find anywhere in the family, including joins of more than two lists, is 2 bits on the strict join in level terms, and 4.1 bits if a disjoint-set join that nobody has is assumed. Both are footnotes under the plan's rule.
2. The sentence "complementary used sets make the test an equality" is true only for the last join of a plan. Every join below it is a disjointness test, which has no hash key. The verdict holds for joins of more than two lists for a different reason, priced in section 1.
3. The claim the memo could not check (tracked subsets are the best sound sketch) is unproven and I did not prove it. It is not needed: a bound that does not depend on the kind of sketch replaces it (section 2). That bound is mine, model level, weakly checked on toys, and has not had a second reader.
4. The join gains 3.0 bits on the model tree, not 5.9. The memo compares a widest level (2^128.7) with a node sum (2^134.6).
5. Neither "2^126.6" nor "2^128.7" is a floor. Section 3 says what each is the price of.
6. The 35-bit seam is not a seam rate. Independent halves collide at 61.9 bits with the real colour counts. Halves with complementary tile sets collide at 42.9 bits, because of an exact parity identity (section 4). No price changes.
7. None of the five papers could be opened by me either. Two attributions are wrong at the level of the abstract (section 5).

## 1. Joins of more than two lists

The memo's argument (complementary sets, so equality, so a hash is optimal) covers exactly one join: the one whose two inputs partition the tiles. In a plan with four lists, the two inner joins combine fillings whose tile sets must be disjoint and whose sizes do not add up to 256. That is set disjointness, not equality. A1 does not apply to it, and the communication bound the memo cites without using (disjointness of two k-sets needs on the order of k bits) says no short key exists for it. So the memo's framing misses the place where a lossy summary of the used set would have to act in a k-list algorithm. The price, with the real geometry (`r4_klist.out`):

**Quadrants, Schroeppel and Shamir.** Four 8x8 quadrant lists of 2^86.6 to 2^95.8 entries. An inner join (two quadrants into a half board) has a seam key of 30.9 bits. Seam-matched pairs: 2^160.7 for the half without the centre clue, 2^151.5 for the other. Only 2^-30.1 (2^-29.5) of them have disjoint tile sets. Guessing a modulus on a linear hash of the partial sums of indicator vectors does not help: over the 2^m guesses, the inner joins output every seam-matched pair exactly once, whatever m is, because the puzzle has one solution and no guess can be skipped. Leaving the disjointness test to the outer join (partial sums allowed to hold a 2, the all-ones condition applied at the end) changes nothing: the same 2^160.7 pairs are produced. Price: **2^160.7**, against 2^128.7 for the two-list join. Modulus guessing buys memory only, as the cryptanalysis survey said.

**Four bands of four rows.** The two inner bands have 2^135.8 and 2^126.7 fillings (no frame row to thin them). One of them already exceeds the tree. Not a plan.

**Wagner's tree.** The four quadrant lists multiply to 2^374.0 and E[Z] is 2^-24.5, so the full condition is 398.6 bits wide and the tree needs about 2^132.9 solutions. The instance has about one.

**Guessing the cross seam first** (dissection style): 123.7 bits of seam before any work per guess.

**Representations** (Howgrave-Graham and Joux). One solution has one representation per way of cutting the board. A family of R cuts gives R representations and R times as many partial objects, so filtering by a hash to keep one representation leaves lists of the mean size, as the survey derived. The re-check's objection (W6: the filtered list cannot be generated without enumerating each region in full) is answered in the knapsack setting by building the filtered lists from quarter lists with modular constraints. Here that is the quadrant join above, at 2^160.7 per cut, for the same reason: disjointness is not a key.

**Every guillotine plan.** I priced all recursive splits of the board into rectangles (18,496 rectangles, every split, leaves generated by the best of four scans), in level terms:

| Joins used below the last one | Cheapest plan | Its shape |
|---|---|---|
| Seam-keyed, disjointness tested pair by pair (what exists) | 2^129.2 | two scans and one equality join: no inner join survives anywhere in the tree |
| Hypothetical join that lists disjoint seam-matched pairs at the cost of its output | 2^124.6 | peel rows one at a time; every plan contains a list of at least 2^124.6 entries (the 11-row band) |

(The 2^129.2 is the best rectangular cut; the memo's 2^128.7 uses a cut in mid-row, which this search does not include.) The share of disjoint pairs explains the first row: 1.1 bits for one cell against 136, 6.6 bits for 32 against 32, 31.3 for 64 against 64, 112 for 104 against 104. A search that adds one cell at a time is the plan that pays the least for disjointness, and it is the tree.

The second row is the cap on the whole direction. Even a perfect disjoint-set join, which would be a breakthrough on orthogonal vectors, takes guillotine plans to 2^124.6: 4.1 bits under the two-list join and 5.6 bits under the tree's widest level. Under the plan's rule that is a footnote.

**Ruling.** "No lossy summary beats the exact two-list join" stands for k lists, with the reason replaced: (a) the equality argument is about the last join only; (b) inner joins are disjointness tests and cost 2^30 or more per halving of the board; (c) with one solution, modulus guessing enumerates everything once; (d) with an ideal inner join the cap is 4.1 bits. Not covered: region shapes that are not rectangles, and lists built from restricted tile pools.

## 2. Are tracked subsets the best sound sketch? A bound that does not need the answer

I could not prove the extremality claim and I do not think it is the right question. Two observations.

First, the memo's counting of pseudo-fillings (A2, A3) does not price a table. If two strict prefixes share a sketch state, the extra accepted fillings are a prefix of one with a suffix of the other, and they reach the same final cell as a strict filling. They add no cell. What merging does is make the final classes coarse.

Second, there is a bound that holds for any sketch.

**Lookahead lemma (derived here, model level).** Take a cut at depth d, a strict top search, and a bottom table built by a DP over (frontier, s), where s is any function of the tile ids fixed without looking at their colours, and sound (the table never rejects a top node that has a strict completion). Let bottom layer j be the state after the j bottom cells farthest from the cut, and D = 256 - j. If the DP holds at most θ N_B(j) states at layer j, then at least about (1 - θ) of the strict nodes at depth D of the top's own scan have an ancestor at depth d that passes the table.

Sketch of proof. Take a strict node at depth D: a top node with tile set U plus a strict extension v over the 256 - d - j cells next to the cut, with tile set V. Let Y be the j tiles left over. Some ordering of Y followed by v is a word of distinct tiles with set "complement of U", so soundness for all instances forces its final key to be the cell the top node looks up. Any legal prefix that reaches the same state as that ordering of Y, at the frontier under v, followed by v, reaches the same cell. So the top node passes as soon as the state (frontier under v, class of Y) is one the DP reached. With tile labels exchangeable, Y is a random set with respect to the classes. If the strict prefixes at a frontier fall into s distinct classes out of μ prefixes, the probability that a random set's class is among them is at least 1 - s/μ (for class weights x_q μ^-1: sum of x(1 - e^-x) is at least sum of (x - 1 + e^-x), since 1 - e^-x (1 + x) is never negative). So a DP that holds a fraction θ of the strict states lets through the ancestors of a fraction 1 - θ of the depth-D nodes, and the search then visits those nodes.

Consequence. With ρ the share let through at layer j, the cost is at least ρ N_A(D) and at least (1 - ρ) N_B(256 - D), so at least their harmonic combination, for every D beyond the cut; and at least the top's widest level up to the cut. Over all cuts (`r5_sketch_bound.out`): **at least 2^126.7**, against 2^128.7 for the strict join and 2^130.2 for the tree's widest level. The two-sided level min(N_A(D), N_B(256 - D)) peaks at 2^127.6 at D = 139, and no sketch gets under half of it.

So one sound sketch table of any kind, tracked subsets or not, saves at most 2.0 bits on the strict join. This also explains the memo's toy finding and its one exception (a small bottom region where the top tree has few extensions).

Toy check of the lemma (`r6_toy_lookahead.out`): seven 5x5 generated boards, tracked subsets, class counts and GF(2) linear sketches, 160 (sketch, layer) pairs at row boundaries. No pair violates g >= 1 - θ (margin 0.05). The test is weak: in only 16 pairs does the sketch DP hold fewer states than the strict DP (θ under 1), and in all 16 the share of depth-D nodes kept is 1.000 to three decimals, with θ from 0.07 to under 1. In the other 144 pairs the sketch DP is larger than the strict one and the lemma says nothing. So the toys show the direction (a DP that merges lets everything through) and do not test the constant. The second 5x5 family stopped after one board and the 6x6 board did not run, on the time guard; four sketch DPs went over the 3 million state cap.

What the lemma does not cover: sketches whose classes are built from the tile colours (the memo already puts these outside, under gaps 1 and 5, and decremental state-space relaxation is exactly that kind); two or more tables at different depths (each extra table can buy about one more bit by the same arithmetic, with returns that stop after two or three); a top side that is also a relaxation. The step "Y is a random set with respect to the classes" is an independence assumption of the same kind as the first-moment model.

**Ruling on the unchecked point.** Unproven, and it should be dropped from the memo. Replace "Price: at least 2^128.7 ... Assumption: tracked subsets are near the best" by "at least 2^126.7 for any sound sketch fixed without looking at the colours (lookahead bound, re-check's derivation, weakly toy-checked, not yet read by a second person)".

## 4. The seam width

Real colour counts (`r2_seam.out`): 17 interior colours with 48 half-edges (5 colours) or 50 (12 colours), 840 in all; 5 frame colours with 24 each. Per interior edge the collision width of two independent half-edges is 4.0870 bits against 4.0875 uniform. The real counts change nothing: a row seam (14 interior and 2 frame edges) is **61.86 bits** for independent halves, a mid-row seam 65.9.

The 35 bits come from the identity N_A N_B = E[Z] x C x (seam). That identity mixes three things:

| Quantity | Row seam | Mid-row seam (d = 137) |
|---|---|---|
| Independent halves, real counts | 61.9 | 65.9 |
| Halves with complementary tile sets, all other edges matched (the model's joint probability) | 42.9 | 46.9 |
| The memo's implied width | 35.0 | 39.0 |

The 19.0 bits between the first two rows are exact and have a one-line cause. **Parity identity:** in a filling of a region with every internal edge matched, each colour appears on the open boundary an even or odd number of times according to its count on the tiles used, because matched edges consume half-edges in pairs. So the parity vector of the seam colours is a linear function over GF(2) of the indicator vector of the used set. On the real tiles the map from tile to colour-parity vector has rank 21 of 22, and every colour count is even, so two halves with complementary sets always have seams of equal parity. The parity vector of a row seam carries 19.0 bits (15.6 interior, 3.4 frame), which is the gap. Toy check (`r3_toy_seam.out`, 21 distinct generated boards, 4x4 and 5x5): the identity is violated on 0 fillings; independent halves collide at 7.07 bits on average (uniform 5.2 to 9.2), pairs with equal seam parity at 3.44, complementary pairs at 3.27.

The remaining 7.9 bits (42.9 to 35.0) are not a seam effect. The memo's identity multiplies the two halves' marginal counts, and under a fixed census the internal matches of two complementary halves are positively dependent. Toys show the same sign: complementary pairs outnumber N_A N_B / C by 1.0 to 3.2 times on the eight 4x4 boards (worked by hand from the counts in `r3_toy_seam.out`, with C = 2,520).

**What it changes.**
- The memo's open question ("which seam width is right for false joins, 61.9 or 35") has an answer: 61.9. False joins under a b-bit hash of the used set are pairs with different sets, and they collide on the seam at the independent rate. The hash width for no false join is 190 bits. The "217" in appendix D is wrong.
- 19 of the 241 bits of the used-set condition are already implied by the seam key. The exact join condition at depth 137 is 280.3 bits, not 241.3 + 66.0.
- No price moves, because every price here is a list size or a level, not a count of false joins.
- The parity vector is a free, exact, 21-bit linear sketch of the used set. It prunes nothing: it also holds for fillings that reuse tiles, so it cannot see a repeat, and a colour-only DP already enforces it.
- Nobody should quote 35 bits as a seam.

## 6. Two proof repairs

**A1.** Let σ take 2^b1 values on top sets and τ' take 2^b2 values on complemented bottom sets, with class sizes a_x and c_y, and n_xy sets in both classes x and y. Every pair of sets in (class x, class y) with n_xy > 0 is accepted. Accepted pairs are at least the sum over such (x, y) of a_x c_y, which is at least the sum of a_x n_xy = sum of a_x^2, at least C^2 / 2^b1; and the same with b2. So the pass rate of a wrong pair is at least 2^-min(b1, b2) - 1/C, and a b-bit hash on each side attains it. The memo's version, with b the two widths added, is true and too weak to show that.

**A2.** Replace "the j-sets whose orderings reach q" by P_q, the ordered injective prefixes that reach q, whose sets number at least |P_q| / j!. From q the automaton accepts at least (256 - j)! |P_q| / j! suffixes. Accepted words are at least (256 - j)! / j! times the sum of |P_q|^2, at least 256! C(256, j) / W_j. Same bound, no assumption on order.

## 7. Proposed wording for O1's scope

"Nor lossy states that are functions of the used set and the frontier fixed without looking at the tile colours (hashes, Bloom filters, tracked subsets, class counts, linear projections, random tile partitions), nor joins of more than two region lists: in the model the first save at most 2.0 bits on the two-list join and the second nothing, 4.1 bits if a disjoint-set join at output cost existed. Graded derived; the bound behind the 2.0 bits has had one reader and a weak toy check. Outside: sketches built from the tile colours."

## 9. Scripts

All run as `python3 -I <script> <official csv>` on one core at `nice -n 10`; the toys take no argument.

- `r_common.py`: loader and model wrapper (imports the re-check's `m12_model.py`, read only).
- `r1_floor.py`, `.out`: list sizes, widest levels and node sums for every cut; three generation orders per side. Under 10 s.
- `r2_seam.py`, `.out`: real colour counts, the three seam widths, the parity map and its rank. Under 5 s.
- `r3_toy_seam.py`, `.out`: toy seam collision rates and the parity identity. About 2 minutes. The 5x5 rows with "cut 3" are mirror images of the "cut 2" rows and add nothing.
- `r4_klist.py`, `.out`: quadrant and band plans, share of disjoint pairs, all guillotine plans with real and ideal joins. 11 s.
- `r5_sketch_bound.py`, `.out`: the lookahead bound per cut. Under 5 s.
- `r6_toy_lookahead.py`, `.out`: toy check of the lemma. 693 s for seven boards; the board loop has a 600 s guard, so the last 5x5 board of the second family and the 6x6 board were not run.

Not done: the memo's three scripts were read, and their outputs used, but not rerun line by line; `r1` and `r4` recompute the figures this review relies on from the model directly.
