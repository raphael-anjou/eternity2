**Headline.** The suggested headline ("the square root is 2^67 and that is the first route that is not absurd on paper") is **half right**. The exponent part holds: quantum backtracking halves 134.4 bits to 67.2 (theorem, query model), which on paper saves more bits than the 56 to 66 bits the classical program is short. The "not absurd" part fails once the published constants go in. Montanaro detection costs about 32 x sqrt(T n) = 2^76.2 walk steps (prefactor from Bai et al. 2023). Each step is at least 10^3 logical T-layers. That gives about 2^87.7 = 10^26.4 serial logical layers and about 10^29 to 10^30 Toffoli gates. At the 10 microsecond reaction time of current surface-code designs, one machine needs about 10^14 years: 10^4 times the age of the universe. That is 13 orders short of a 10-year run, and 21 to 22 orders above the gate budget of the largest roadmapped machine (IBM Starling, 2029: 10^8 gates on 200 logical qubits). Splitting the tree across K machines buys only sqrt(K), so 10 years needs about 10^26 fault-tolerant machines. The quantum route ends about as far away as the classical one (17 to 20 orders). It only gets there by a different road.

## 1. The analog

**Quantum backtracking (Montanaro 2015/2018, refined by Ambainis and Kokainis 2017, Jarret and Wan 2018).** This is the closest exact analog in any field I surveyed, because it takes a *classical backtracking tree as given*. The input is the same object the program has measured: a predicate P (is this partial board a dead end, a solution, or undecided) and a heuristic h (which cell to fill next). The tree has T = 10^40.457 = 2^134.4 nodes and depth n = 256. Mapping:
- Tiles and rotations: the child set of a node (the tiles x rotations that fit the next cell in row-scan order). Branching degree d is the maximum child count.
- All-different: lives inside P. The quantum state carries the whole partial assignment (cell to tile and rotation) plus a used-tile register, so the "state must carry the used set" obstacle that kills transfer matrices costs only memory here: about 256 x 10 qubits plus ancillas, a few thousand to 10^5 logical qubits (argued, by analogy with Campbell et al.'s "more than 10^5 logical qubits" for colouring).
- Planted solution: the marked vertex. With five clues there is about 1 solution, so the multi-solution refinements (Jarret-Wan's k marked vertices) do not help.
- Density: enters only through T. The algorithm is oblivious to it.

**Grover on the frame / nested search.** This means Grover over the 10^32.4 frames that survive the corner lists (`M12/frame-information.md`), with an oracle that solves the interior. Nested quantum search costs the square root of the classical nested cost. Frames x pass rate x interior nodes = 10^32.4 x 10^-5.2 x 10^13.5 = 10^40.7, which is T again, so the cost is about 10^20.35 (argued). Splitting the tree differently changes nothing. The quantum cost is sqrt of the classical tree whatever the decomposition.

**Quantum annealing, QAOA, coherent Ising machines, digital annealers, memcomputing.** These minimise a QUBO/Ising energy. Eternity II maps to a one-hot QUBO with 256 x 256 x 4 = 262,144 binary variables before embedding (`M12/literature-2026.md` section 4.6). All-different becomes a quadratic penalty per tile and per cell, and the planted solution is a zero-energy ground state. **The analogy breaks** on two counts. These are heuristics with no completeness guarantee, the same family as simulated annealing, which ROUTES already covers under "Local search". And the penalty encoding makes the landscape a constrained spin glass with mostly invalid configurations.

**DNA / algorithmic tile self-assembly.** This is literally edge matching: Wang tiles with sticky-end "glues" attach where their edges match (Winfree's abstract Tile Assembly Model). **The analogy breaks on all-different.** A test tube holds about 10^13 copies of each tile type, so an assembly can reuse a tile freely: it solves the Wang tiling problem with replacement, not Eternity II. Nondeterministic growth (several tiles competing for a site) is a *random probe* of the same backtracking tree, not a parallel exhaustive search. Each assembly follows one root-to-leaf path.

**FPGA / ASIC / GPU backtracking.** These are the same algorithm on faster silicon. The analog is exact, and the arithmetic is a node rate.

## 3. Hardness evidence

1. **Square-root barrier for quantum search (theorem; applies).** BBBV-type optimality (Zalka 1999) and the Omega(sqrt(T n)) tree bound (Aaronson-Ambainis, quoted in Montanaro 2018 section 5) say that no quantum algorithm with only oracle access to the tree beats sqrt(T). Assumptions: query model, unstructured or worst-case tree. They hold for any algorithm that treats P as a black box. A quantum algorithm that exploits structure is not excluded, but none exists for edge matching. **So the best quantum exponent available is 67.2 bits, and every decomposition (frames first, nested Grover) lands on the same 10^20.2.**
2. **No parallel speedup beyond sqrt(K) (theorem; applies).** Zalka 1999. Classical search parallelises perfectly over subtrees. Quantum gains only sqrt(K) from K machines. This turns the time gap into a squared machine-count gap.
3. **Quadratic speedups do not survive fault-tolerance constants at early scale (estimate; applies a fortiori).** Babbush et al. 2021 and Campbell et al. 2019. Their assumptions (surface code, 10^-3 error, 1 microsecond cycle) are today's best published operating point. They are generous to the quantum side (one Toffoli matched against one classical clock cycle). A row-scan node costs 10 ns on a core, and a walk step costs 10^3+ logical layers.
4. **Adiabatic optimisation on random NP-complete instances (argued in the paper, not a theorem).** Altshuler, Krovi, Roland, PNAS 2010, arXiv:0912.0746: "exponentially small gaps appear close to the end of the adiabatic algorithm"; "the system gets trapped in one of the numerous local minima". Assumptions: large random instances (exact cover style). Eternity II is fixed-size, planted and at the critical density, which is the regime where the many-local-minima picture is expected. I have no evidence that it holds or fails at n = 256 tiles.
5. **Locality barrier for low-depth QAOA (theorem, asymptotic).** Farhi, Gamarnik, Gutmann, arXiv:2004.09002: for random bounded-degree graphs, QAOA with depth p below about a constant times log n cannot beat 0.854 of optimum, through the Overlap Gap Property. The authors note that "even at one million qubits we can only show that the algorithm is blocked if p is in single digits". Assumptions: random sparse graphs, n to infinity. Eternity II is a fixed grid, and the OGP has not been established for edge matching. **Transfers as an expectation, not a proof.**
6. **Annealing hardware results on structured CSPs are negative at this scale (experiment).** 64 tiles (Seifer), a 40-cell tetromino board through hybrid decomposition (Eagle et al.), exact cover failing at 120 logical qubits on DW2000Q (Willsch et al.), and no digital-annealer speedup on sparse 2D problems (Aramon et al.). Eternity II needs 262,144 variables. The largest positive annealing experiment I found on any tiling or edge-matching problem is about 3 orders of magnitude smaller in variable count.

## 4. Transfer candidates

Nothing in this field transfers as a route to a solution this century. One desk item is worth doing, because it retires a row with a number instead of a sentence.

### T1. Price quantum backtracking on the real tree (desk exercise, no run)

*Statement.* Montanaro detection on the row-scan tree with five clues costs L = 32 x sqrt(T n) x D logical T-layers, where D is the T-depth of one walk step (the P and h evaluations for an Eternity II node, plus diffusion over up to d children).

*Bits.*
- 32: phase-estimation prefactor (Bai et al. 2023): 5 bits.
- sqrt(T) = 2^(134.4 / 2): 67.2 bits.
- sqrt(n) = sqrt(256): 4 bits.
- D: 10^3 to 10^5, so 10 to 16.6 bits. **This factor is a guess.** The low end is Campbell et al.'s colouring step (3,114 T-depth). The high end allows for Bai et al.'s 8 d^2 log term with d about 16.
- Total: L = 2^86.2 to 2^92.8, central 2^87.7 = 10^26.4.
- Search (not detection) adds up to 8 bits with Montanaro's descent, or about log factors with Jarret-Wan.

*Against time.* A 10-year run affords 2^44.8 layers at the 10 microsecond reaction time (Gidney-Ekerå), 2^52.5 at Campbell's "realistic" 50 ns, and 2^59.1 at the "optimistic" 0.5 ns. The gaps are 42.9, 35.3 and 28.6 bits: 12.9, 10.6 and 8.6 orders. With K machines on disjoint subtrees, time scales as sqrt(T/K), so closing the gap needs K = 10^25.8, 10^21.2 and 10^17.2 machines.

*Against gates.* Toffoli count is about 2^76.2 walk steps x 10^6 to 2.5 x 10^7 per step = 10^29 to 10^30.3. Starling's budget is 10^8, a gap of 21 to 22 orders. Blue Jay is reported in press at 10^9; I did not confirm that on the IBM page.

*Comparison with classical.* Classical machine-years are 10^25.0 on one 10^8 nodes/s core, 10^23.0 on one 10^10 nodes/s GPU, and 10^20.9 for the whole volunteer fleet at 1.1 x 10^12 nodes/s. Even an ASIC fabric running at the entire Bitcoin network's hash rate (about 10^21 operations/s, `M12/solve-path-5-price.md`) needs 10^12 years. Quantum on one machine needs 10^13.9 years (reaction-limited) and 10^9.6 years (optimistic). Per machine the quantum route is 10^9 to 10^13 times fewer machine-years than a core. But the machine does not exist, and it does not parallelise.

*Cheapest experiment that could kill it.* Compile one walk step of the Eternity II node predicate in an open resource estimator (Qualtran, or Azure's resource estimator) and read off D and the logical qubit count. This takes about a day of desk work, one core and no run. Kill line: D above 10^3. The central figure already assumes D = 3,000, so it would only move the answer between 10^13 and 10^16 years. *Odds that it changes the verdict: under 1%.* Its value is a priced row, not a route.

### T2 and T3. None

- **Annealers, CIM, digital annealer, memcomputing**: heuristics in the local-search family, already "killed (saturated per EXPLORED)" in the ROUTES "Local search" row. The published hardware reaches 64 tiles at best, and Eternity II's sparse grid QUBO is the regime where the digital annealer showed no speedup. There is no testable claim worth a slot.
- **DNA tile self-assembly**: violates all-different, because copies are unlimited. A probe-based physical sampler would need at least as many correct assemblies as there are random probes required to hit the planted path. A tube holds about 10^13 to 10^14 copies per tile type at 100 nM, and the per-tile error of 1/3,000 gives (1 - 1/3000)^256 = 0.92 correct boards. So error is not the binding constraint. Sample count is, by roughly 20+ orders (argued; the probe hit rate on the real tree is not computed here).
- **FPGA/ASIC/GPU**: no new transfer. This is the ROUTES Hardware row and A8; every credible multiplier together is worth about 2.5 orders against a 17 to 20 order gap.
