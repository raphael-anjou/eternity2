# Citation audit of the eight field surveys of 2026-11-13

Second analysis: citation check only (does the source exist, with these authors, year and venue, and does it say what the survey says it says). The surveys' own derivations are out of scope. Written 2026-11-13.

Method. For each survey: every citation that carries a headline claim (final summary, "hardness evidence", "transfer candidates"), plus at least five sampled from its table. Each source was opened today: arXiv PDFs and other PDFs were downloaded and converted to text locally (pdftotext) and the relied-on sentence was found by text search; arXiv abstracts came from the arXiv API; IACR ePrint abstract pages were fetched directly, and ePrint, IACR archive and HAL PDFs that refuse direct download were read from web.archive.org snapshots. "Confirmed" means I found the statement in the source text myself and quote it with its location. "Abstract" means only the abstract was checked.

Verdict scale: confirmed; confirmed, different scope; could not open; misattributed; not found.

## 1. Claims to correct (misattributed, not found, or wider than the source)

None of the checked citations is fabricated. Every work exists with the authors, year and venue given, apart from one date inconsistency. The problems are misreadings of tables, one mislabelled bound, and three claims stated more broadly than the source.

1. **sat-cp-proof-complexity, section 2.1, Salassa et al. row: table misread.** As written: "META 2010 contest instances (10x10 to 16x16): this paper 458 on the 16x16 contest board in 360 min; Coelho et al. 425 on 14x14." What Table 5 of arXiv:1709.00252 actually shows: for the present paper (100 runs), 457 on the 16x16 contest instance (360 min) and 458 on the official EII instance (360 min); Coelho et al. reach 425 on the **16x16** instance (60 min) and 325 on the 14x14. The same table also lists Wauters et al. at 460 (16x16) and 461 (EII), above this paper. Corrected sentence: "META 2010 contest instances (10x10 to 16x16): this paper 457 on the 16x16 contest board and 458 on the official board, both in 360 min; Coelho et al. 425 on the 16x16 board in 60 min; Wauters et al. 460 and 461; Verhaard 467 on the official board." A smaller point in the same row: the quoted "Instances of size 7 x 7 could not be solved completely" is about the max-clique formulation. The paper says the MILP solves only up to 6x6 ("The MILP is also able to solve up to size 6 × 6").

2. **exact-algorithms, section 2c, Bandwidth row: misattributed bound.** As written: "Bandwidth | O(4.383^n) time and space (Distortion; Bandwidth in O(9.363^n) polynomial space, earlier O*(10^n))". In Cygan and Pilipczuk (arXiv:1004.5012), O(4.383^n) time and space is the bound for **Distortion**. For Bandwidth the paper gives O(9.363^n) in polynomial space, improving Feige and Kilian's O*(10^n). Corrected sentence: "Bandwidth: O(9.363^n) time in polynomial space (improving O*(10^n)); the same paper gives O(4.383^n) time and space for Distortion." The derived "base above 2" conclusion still holds.

3. **learning-guided-search, sections 4.1 (point 2) and 4.3: NeuroCore ablation stated more broadly than the source.** As written: "NeuroCore's vanished under a random-score control" and "NeuroCore's own ablation says the benefit was the refocusing, not the network". The source (Selsam and Bjørner, arXiv:1903.04671 v7, "Ablations", p. 12 to 13) splits by benchmark set. On SATCOMP-2018, "rand-glucose solved the same number of problems that neuro-glucose did" and "most (if not all) of the benefit came from the periodic refocusing". On the in-distribution private scheduling set, "rand-glucose solved 2.5% fewer problems than glucose (while neuro-glucose solved 20% more ...)", and the authors conclude "there was indeed substantial signal in the NeuroCore" scores there. Corrected sentence: "NeuroCore's gain on SATCOMP-2018 was matched by random scores (the benefit there was the periodic refocusing); on the in-distribution scheduling set the network scores did carry signal (+20% against -2.5% for random scores)." Section 3.1 item 3 and the section 2 table row are worded correctly ("random scores equal on SATCOMP-2018").

4. **monte-carlo-rare-events, bottom line (first paragraph): Chatterjee and Diaconis applied more broadly than Theorem 1.1.** As written: "For every particle or importance-sampling method the matching result is that the sample size needed is about exp(KL divergence from the target to the proposal) (Chatterjee, Diaconis 2018, Theorem 1.1)." Theorem 1.1 (arXiv:1511.01437, p. 3) is about the L1 error of the plain importance-sampling estimator I_n(f) built from n i.i.d. draws from one fixed proposal mu. The theorem does not cover adaptive particle methods (splitting, SMC with resampling, population annealing, cross-entropy). The same paper also says that for a rare event A the relevant size is exp(D(nu_A || mu)), not exp(D(nu || mu)) (Theorem 1.3). Section 3, B3, correctly labels the extension to search and to adaptive methods as **argued**, but the bottom line does not. Corrected sentence: "For plain importance sampling from a fixed proposal, the sample size needed is about exp(KL divergence from the target to the proposal) (Chatterjee and Diaconis 2018, Theorem 1.1); this survey argues (B3) that adaptive particle methods pay the same price for a single planted solution."

5. **quantum-unconventional, table row and hardness item 6: Seifer reported selectively.** Seifer (2026) exists: Shahar Seifer (Weizmann Institute), "A Pipeline for Solving Edge-Matching Puzzles and Their Implications for Protein Folding", bioRxiv, posted 24 May 2026, doi:10.64898/2026.05.23.727379. The abstract confirms the two facts the survey uses: the D-Wave hybrid solver "demonstrates a complete solution only for puzzle instances up to 64 tiles", and "Simulated quantum annealing fails on this benchmark" while "an original classical heuristic, 'nucleation with deduction', succeeds". The survey leaves out the abstract's main positive claim. A multistage pipeline "based on multiple hybrid optimization steps" was run on "a puzzle comparable in complexity to Eternity II and with a known solution". Its optimisation problem "involves ∼100,000 logical variables and requires partial initialization", and the author writes that the problem "exceeds the capacity of current quantum annealing processors but lies within reach of hybrid quantum-classical solvers". The survey therefore understates the source, and it conflicts with item 6's "largest positive annealing experiment I found ... about 3 orders of magnitude smaller in variable count". This is an not independently checked preprint, and the abstract does not say how many tiles the test puzzle had, how much was initialised, or what share of the work the annealer did. Corrected sentence for item 6: "Seifer (2026, bioRxiv, not independently checked) reports a complete hybrid D-Wave solve only up to 64 tiles, and a hybrid quantum-classical pipeline of about 10^5 logical variables, with partial initialisation, that solved a test puzzle 'comparable in complexity to Eternity II'. The abstract gives neither the test puzzle's size, nor the share of the work done by the annealer, nor what was initialised." The full text should be read before the site cites this preprint either way.

6. **quantum-unconventional, table row "Fault-tolerant cost of backtracking on random CSPs": qubit count quoted at its low end.** As written: "about 10^12 physical qubits". Tables 4 to 6 of Campbell, Khurana and Montanaro (Quantum 3, 167) give factory qubits from 1.38 x 10^12 (optimistic) to 3.85 x 10^14 (realistic 12-SAT). Corrected: "10^12 to 4 x 10^14 physical qubits, factory-dominated, depending on the regime". The rest of the row matches the paper.

7. **tilings-jigsaws, section 0 item 1: date.** "Heule 2009" here, against "Heule (2008)" in sat-cp-proof-complexity. The PDF (cs.cmu.edu/~mheule/publications/eternity.pdf) carries no date and cites nothing later than 2008. It is usually cited as the LaSh 2008 workshop paper. Use 2008 in both surveys.
