## 1. The analog

### 1.1 The closest objects in the field

| Eternity II | Analog in the field | Sense of the match |
|---|---|---|

The single published statistical-physics treatment of planted tile matching is Hamze et al. (2018), already in `2026-11-12/notes/literature-2026.md`. Reading its text today adds one fact that limits its use here: its authors state that their puzzles "differ in a key way from conventional puzzles such as Eternity II", because in Eternity II each tile may go to more than one board location whereas in their construction each site has its own allowed set [C]. Their model has no each-tile-once constraint. It is a lattice CSP with site-dependent domains, not a permutation problem.

### 1.2 Where the analogy breaks

1. **Geometry.** Every theorem and every cavity computation in the table of section 2 is for sparse random (locally tree-like) factor graphs or fully connected models, in the limit N to infinity. Eternity II is a 16x16 lattice with loops of length 4 everywhere, plus one global constraint. I found no rigorous or cavity treatment of a random edge-matching ensemble with a permutation constraint (searches listed in section 5).
2. **Size.** N = 256 is fixed. Thresholds, "with high probability" and "exponential in N" have no literal meaning for one instance. The repo's own measurement (30 of 39 digits at the peak are frame multiplicity) says the instance is boundary-dominated: 60 of 256 cells are on the frame and the frame alone has 2^124.9 placements (A5), against a total price of 2^134.4.
3. **What is hard.** [D] Without the permutation constraint the model is a 17-state vertex model on a 16-wide strip, exactly contractible with a frontier of 5^2 x 17^14 = 2^61.9 states (2^65.9 with the in-row leg, the path 1 figure). So everything between 2^66 and 2^134 is caused by the permutation constraint, which is not a finite-dimensional object. The right universality class is therefore not "2D CSP" (where treewidth caps the cost at exp(O(sqrt N))) but "mean-field assignment problem whose pair costs happen to be arranged on a lattice". This is why finite-dimensional results (BP on lattices, tensor contraction) help with the part of the problem that is already cheap.
4. **The planting protocol is not known.** The ensemble statements assume a uniform painter given the colour census. ROUTES ("Traces of the maker's program") leaves a coarse-stratified painter as an untestable loophole.

## 3. Hardness evidence

### 3.1 Planted versus random: what the field supports for this instance

**[C]** The planted ensemble is the random ensemble reweighted by the number of solutions (row 3, proof of Proposition 4.4). When the solution count concentrates, the two are equivalent and the planted solution is one typical solution among the others (rows 1 to 3).

**[D] Chi-square form for a finite instance.** Let P_0 be the null distribution over tile sets (independent edge colours given the census, the repo's first-moment model) and P_pl the planted one (uniform painter). Then P_pl(T) = P_0(T) Z(T) / E_0[Z], hence chi^2(P_pl || P_0) = E_0[Z^2]/E_0[Z]^2 - 1 = E_pl[Z]/E_0[Z] - 1, and total variation is at most half the square root of that. The theory re-check's U4 gives E_pl[Z] = 1 + E[Z] + about 0.03 in the same model (graded "not verified, 0.5" by its reviewer; intermediate difference sets are bounded by argument, not summed).

- With the starter tile pinned, E[Z] = 14,702.27: chi^2 = 1.03 / 14,702 = 7.0e-5, total variation at most 0.5 x sqrt(7.0e-5) = 4.2e-3.
- With no tile pinned, E[Z] = 1.1527e7; the planted board and its three rotations give E_pl[Z] about 4 + E[Z], so chi^2 is about 4 / 1.15e7 = 3.5e-7 and total variation at most 3e-4.

Reading: if U4 is right, then under the uniform-painter assumption no statistic of the piece set, of any complexity, can tell the official set from a random set with the same census with advantage above about 0.4 percent, and (by Cauchy-Schwarz) any event of probability p under the null has probability at most sqrt(p (1 + chi^2)) under planting. The field calls this quiet planting; here it is far inside the quiet regime because the expected count is in the thousands to millions, not near 1. This is consistent with three things the program measured separately: Track 10 (the tree is that of a random instance to 0.03 digits, the official set is ordinary among 20 painted boards), the 31 null statistics of the "Traces of the maker's program" row, and the A1 kill (no search-free statistic beats colour rarity). It upgrades them from "we looked and saw nothing" to "nothing can be there", conditional on U4 and on the uniform painter. It does not cover a non-uniform painter (the stratified-painter loophole stays where ROUTES left it).

**[D] Consequence for the problem statement.** A planted-inference gap in the field's sense (rows 15 to 17) is a gap between detecting or recovering a planted signal and doing so efficiently. Without clues there is no signal at all: the constructed solution is exchangeable with about 10^7 others. So Eternity II without clues is not a planted-recovery problem; it is "find any solution of a typical random instance at entropy ln(1.15e7)/256 = 0.064 nats per cell", which is 1.26 percent of the log-volume of the placement space (ln(4! 56! 196! 4^196) = 1289.3 nats). The five clues are five oracle-decimation steps on one uniformly chosen solution; they cut the expected count of other solutions from 1.15e7 to 4.1e-8, that is 48 bits of selection, and the repo's prices (10^50.04 without clues, 10^40.46 with five) say they are worth 31.8 bits of search. The field's planted-recovery algorithms (spectral, AMP, low-degree estimators) have nothing to work on except those five tiles.

### 3.2 The instance sits where every known non-exhaustive algorithm class fails on the analog

**[C]** In locked problems, clusters are single solutions; BP decimation "fails completely", survey propagation has no advantage over BP, BP reinforcement and stochastic local search work only in the non-clustered (liquid) phase, and the clustered phase extends over a broad range of densities below the satisfiability threshold (row 4). Planted locked instances are hard between the dynamical threshold and the Kesten-Stigum spinodal, and that range includes densities at and beyond the satisfiability threshold where the planted solution is unique (row 3). In frozen clusters "no existing algorithm" finds solutions in polynomial time (row 1).

**[D] Does Eternity II satisfy the locked condition?** In the edge-colour representation every internal edge variable is in exactly two cell constraints (condition b holds). Condition (a) asks that no two allowed tuples of a constraint differ in a single variable, that is, no two (tile, rotation) pairs agree on three sides. Under the exchangeable model the expected number of such interior pairs is C(196,2) x 4 x 4 / 17^3 = 62, so the cell constraint alone is not strictly locked. The each-tile-once constraint restores it at the level of solutions: using a three-side twin requires the twin to be free, which forces a closed chain of replacements, and the theory re-check's U4 puts the expected number of solution pairs differing on any compact set below 196 cells far below 1. So: locked in effect (isolated solutions), not by the letter of the definition. The count of three-side twins in the real set is a one-line check I did not run (no tile data opened in this survey).

**[D] Assumptions that fail.** These results are cavity computations and experiments on sparse random graphs with N from 10^4 to 10^5. They say nothing rigorous about a 16x16 lattice. What transfers is the mechanism: isolated solutions, an uninformative BP fixed point that is locally stable, and no local basin around a solution. The repo's own data show each mechanism on the real instance: local reasoning sees nothing beyond the corners ("Cheap information about the frame"), the 470 boards sit 246 to 254 cells from any solution (path 4), and local search saturates near 460 to 470.

### 3.3 The overlap gap: what the literature supports about A10 and its non-transfer claim

ROUTES disagreement 10 is between the A10 kill (5x5 and 6x6: the mid-overlap band is cheaper than the far band) and path 4's first-moment model (the sign flips between 8x8 and 12x12; a moat 15 to 20 mismatches high at 16x16).

1. **[C]** The OGP is a statement about an ensemble as N grows, and what it rules out is stable algorithms (rows 11, 12). **[D]** A measurement at 25 or 36 cells cannot confirm or refute it. On a 5x5 board 16 of 25 cells are on the frame; a "mid-overlap" configuration there is a boundary object. The A10 kill is correct as a statement about those sizes and carries no information about 16x16 in either direction. The literature supports "does not transfer" in this weak sense with no reservation.
2. **[C]** What A10 measured, the fewest breaks at each distance from the planted solution, is the planted pairwise form. In the literature that form "only yields lower bounds against local Monte Carlo Markov Chain type algorithms" (row 13). **[D]** So even a clean barrier at 16x16 would bind local search and annealing started far from the solution, which ROUTES already lists as saturated. It would say nothing about backtracking, which is not a stable algorithm, and nothing about the 2^134.4 price.
3. **[D]** Path 4's method is the standard one. The planted-model OGP results obtain the curve "best objective at overlap a with the planted solution" from first and second moments. A first-moment count far below 1 is, by Markov's inequality, a valid high-probability statement that no such board exists under the model; the converse (a board exists wherever the expected count is at least 1) needs a second moment that path 4 does not have. So the moat's height is supported as a lower bound in expectation over the model, its floor is not, and neither is a statement about the one real instance. The literature supports the non-transfer claim in this stronger sense only up to that limit.
4. **[D]** A structural difference from mean-field OGP. There the forbidden overlap band and the energy barrier are extensive in N. On a lattice the cost of replacing a compact region is an interface cost that scales with the region's perimeter, not its area (path 4's moat peaks at 65 to 96 changed cells, about a 9x9 block with perimeter 36, and falls beyond). A sub-extensive barrier of 15 to 20 edges on a 480-edge board is not what stops local search. What stops it is entropy: about 10^9 boards at 470 per solution lie near a solution against 10^33 in total (path 4). The field's name for that is a golf-course landscape of isolated solutions (3.2), and it does not need an overlap gap. I found no OGP result for any finite-dimensional or lattice model (search listed in section 5), so there is no theorem to import either way.
5. **[C]** The OGP is not a reliable predictor on its own: shortest path has it and is easy (row 14). **[D]** The planned two-solution measurement on 6x6 and 7x7 is the right analog of the classical (unplanted) pairwise OGP, but by point 1 it is at sizes where the answer does not bear on 16x16, and by point 2 its best outcome binds a class of algorithms already written off. My reading: A10 and its successor are settled as far as the literature can settle them, and neither outcome changes the price of a solve. I would not spend the two-solution run.

### 3.4 Low-degree and statistical-query barriers

**[C]** Rows 15 to 17. **[D]** Assumptions against this instance: these are asymptotic statements about detection or recovery of a planted signal against a null. By 3.1 the no-clue detection problem here is information-theoretically impossible (total variation at most 3e-4), which is stronger than any low-degree bound: no polynomial of any degree in the tile data can carry information about the planted solution. With clues the question becomes search for a rare object, where the low-degree framework applies only through its use against "low-degree algorithms that output a solution" (row 12), again an asymptotic class statement on random k-SAT. The class includes BP- and SP-guided decimation and local algorithms; it does not include backtracking. Row 16 shows that a hidden permutation does not escape these barriers. None of these results is a lower bound on exhaustive search, and none prices 2^134.4.

### 3.5 What the field says about exhaustive search

**[C]** Row 22 (not independently checked manuscript): on planted jigsaws tuned to an exponential number of solutions, backtracking is exponential for every fill order, and for different orders the blow-up has different causes. Row 5: locked constraints give exhaustive solvers more implications per assignment, so branch-and-bound is not necessarily worse than on k-SAT. **[D]** The official instance is in the many-solutions regime before clues (10^7) and at the threshold after, so the easy-order theorem of row 22 (corner rectangle, branching 1 + Theta(eps)) does not apply; this agrees with ROUTES. The field has no finer statement: the constant in the exponent of backtracking at threshold is not predicted by any theory I could open, and the repo's measured 134.4 bits per 256 cells (0.525 bits per cell) is, as far as I can tell, better data than the literature holds for this model.

### 3.6 Seeding: why five clues do not nucleate

**[C]** In first-order hard phases the known way through is a designed seed from which the solution grows (row 25). **[D]** For a growth front to advance on its own from a seed, a cell with two fixed neighbours must have fewer than one expected wrong candidate. In the interior that number is (196 - k) x 4 / 17^2 with k interior tiles already placed: 2.71 at k = 0, 1.88 at k = 60, 1.33 at k = 100, and it falls below 1 only at k = 124. A seed is supercritical only once about 124 of the 196 interior tiles are placed correctly, or when the true frame is given (the repo's 10^8.3 interior cost). Five tiles, four of them isolated in the interior, are subcritical by a wide margin. The critical nucleus costs as much as the problem. Nothing in the nucleation literature lowers that, because there the designer chooses the couplings to make the seed supercritical and here the couplings are fixed.

### 3.7 Tensor networks and the each-tile-once constraint

**[C]** Exact contraction solves 2D lattice counting and optimisation at sizes above 16x16 for small local dimension (row 20: 32x32 Ising, 18x18 three-state Potts), and counts by "size" are obtained by contracting over a polynomial ring in one fugacity (generic tensor networks). **[D]** A constraint "exactly n_t tiles of type t" for a bounded number of types costs one fugacity per type and stays polynomial; each-tile-once is 256 types with n_t = 1, and coefficient extraction over 256 variables is the permanent-like step path 1 priced at 2^256 contractions or a 10^103-sample sign problem. I found no tensor-network paper that treats an all-different or permutation constraint (search in section 5); the scheduling paper that the search returned compresses d independent constraints into bond dimension d + 1, which is the bounded-types case. Message passing can carry a matching constraint at Bethe level (row 18), which is the saddle-point evaluation of the same coefficient: it reproduces the first-moment count, not the instance (path 1 found the linear per-piece prices have zero margin on toys). Approximate contraction (boundary matrix-product states, corner transfer matrices) would lift path 1's 2^66 memory wall for the colour-only model, but that model overcounts by 10^107.65 per solution and is vacuous as a bound until about 110 cells remain (path 1, section 2.4), so nothing is gained. Verdict: the literature confirms the "Tensor networks ..." row and adds no object smaller than the tree.

## 4. Transfer candidates

No method from this field transfers as a solver with odds I can defend above one in a thousand. The field's main output for this problem is on the barrier side (section 3). Two items are testable and cheap; both are instruments that close open rows with data, not routes to a solution.

### 4.1 BP with an each-tile-once factor on the frame (predicted null)

**Statement.** Run sum-product on the cell-variable factor graph of the real pieces (variables: tile and rotation per cell; factors: 480 edge-match factors and one matching factor per piece type, handled at Bethe level as in row 18), with the five clues pinned, and read the marginals of the 60 frame cells. Claim to test: these marginals carry at least 10 bits about the true frame beyond the 17.45 corner bits the program already has.

**Cost in bits.** If BP marginals carry I bits of information about the frame in total, a frame-ordered search gains at most I bits: cost >= 134.4 - I + log2(overhead). Used once at the root as a static order the overhead is zero. ROUTES asks for "a source of about 56 bits", that is I / 60 = 0.93 bits per frame cell out of a maximum of log2(56) = 5.8. The guessed factor is I. The literature's prediction is I near 0 away from the corners: the uniform fixed point is locally stable in the hard phase (rows 1, 3), BP decimation fails in locked problems (row 4), and on a lattice plain BP behaves as on a random regular graph (row 9). My estimate: I between 0 and 3 bits beyond the corners, so cost 131 to 134 bits.

**Cheapest kill.** On 30 generated framed 8x8 and 10x10 boards at the critical colour count, with the planted solution known: mean log2-rank improvement of the true tile at each frame cell under BP marginals, against arc consistency alone. Kill if the mean gain per non-corner frame cell is below 0.05 bits, or if BP does not converge with damping on more than half the boards. Then one run on the real pieces (no ground truth; report entropy of frame marginals only). A few core-minutes.

**Odds.** 10 or more new bits: 2 percent. 56 bits: below 1 in 10^4. Value of a run: it replaces the "Survey propagation and belief-propagation ordering" row (one 4x4, no committed data) by a measured null with the field's explanation attached.

### 4.2 The contiguity bound as a measured quantity

**Statement.** Var(Z) / E[Z] is of order 1 (U4 says 1.03 with one tile pinned) on generated framed boards across the size ladder, so chi^2(planted || null) = Var(Z)/E[Z]^2 is about 1/E[Z].

**Cost in bits.** None gained: this is a barrier, 134.4 stays. It turns the "Traces of the maker's program" row from 31 null statistics into a bound on every statistic (advantage at most 0.5 x sqrt(c / E[Z]), about 0.4 percent at E[Z] = 14,702), under the uniform-painter assumption.

**Cheapest kill.** Exact enumeration of all solutions on 200 generated boards per rung at 5x5 and 6x6 with palettes giving E[Z] between 5 and 500 (the A10 follow-up already planned such palettes), null boards drawn from the census model. Kill if Var(Z)/E[Z] exceeds 10 or grows with size. Under one core-hour with the existing enumerators.

**Odds.** Holds within a factor 10: 70 percent. The risk is the intermediate difference sets that U4 bounds by argument (border and corner swaps are the largest known terms).

### 4.3 Not proposed, with the reason

- Survey propagation: no advantage over BP when clusters are single solutions (row 4).
- Population annealing with family entropy (row 23, on the "Looks untried" list): an instrument for comparing instance classes; by 3.1 the official instance is a typical member of its class, so the readout would say "typical".
- The two-solution overlap measurement: see 3.3 point 5.
- Any tensor-network variant: see 3.7.
