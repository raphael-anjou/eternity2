There is a tempting shortcut for Eternity II. The border ring is the most
constrained part of the board: sixty cells over only five ring colours, filled
by the four corner pieces and the fifty-six edge pieces (the
[ring-purity page](/research/why/ring-purity) shows why those five colours never
leave the rim). If a fully-matched border is what pins the top of the board,
then a *different* fully-matched border should pin a *different* high interior,
and building several distinct strong borders would open several distinct high
basins. Border-first diversification would be a lever for scores.

This page tests that shortcut with one fixed interior producer. Generating many
structurally distinct fully-matched borders and completing each one with the
same break-tolerant beam gives tops that all land in the same mid-430s band. A
control that completes each border several times with different seeds shows
that the spread between borders is no larger than the spread between seeds on
one border: with this producer, the border choice did not visibly change the
score. That is a statement about this producer, whose ceiling sits near 440
whatever border it gets; whether a near-record producer would see a border
effect is not tested here.

Every score on this page is under the strict five-clue convention (all five
official clues obeyed, matched non-grey adjacencies out of 480); the five clues
sit at interior cells, none on the ring, so every completion here is a legal
strict-5/5 board and the border is hint-unconstrained. The value 455 used below
is a fixed high-band threshold carried over from the source study, not the
record: the current strict-five-clue record is well above it, and the numbers
live on the [records page](/research/records).

## Why the frame might be a loose object

The reason to doubt the shortcut is structural. The border ring is a
self-contained cyclic edge-matching problem over five colours with balanced
supply, and fully-matched borders are easy to generate: the randomised search
below finds a new one on almost every attempt. They couple to the interior
through 56 inward-facing colours (one per edge piece; the corners have none),
a boundary word the interior has to meet, over a 196-cell interior painted
with 17 colours. That channel is thin compared with the interior, which is a
reason to expect the frame to carry little information about the interior's
ceiling. It is an argument, not a measurement; the experiment below is the
measurement.

## What was measured

On the official 256-piece set with the five official clues pinned:

1. generate N structurally distinct fully-matched borders from scratch (a
   randomised depth-first placement over the sixty border cells, grey outward,
   every ring adjacency matched, deduplicated by exact ring equality);
2. freeze each border and fill its 196 interior cells with **one** fixed
   producer at a fixed budget: a break-tolerant beam that fills the interior
   row by row, scoring each candidate by matched-minus-mismatched edges against
   its placed neighbours (the frozen border included) and pruning to a fixed
   width with a seeded tie-break;
3. report the resulting full-board score band, the pairwise interior tile-Hamming
   distance between the best completions, and whether any border reaches the
   455 high-band threshold.

### The main run

Sixteen structurally distinct fully-matched borders, interior beam width 128,
three seeds per border, best kept:

| Quantity | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Distinct fully-matched borders generated | 16 (from 17 depth-first attempts) |
| Top score band (min / median / max) | 434 / 437 / 440 |
| Any border reaches the 455 threshold | no |
| Gap from the best top to 455 | 15 |
| Pairwise interior tile-Hamming (of 196 cells) | 188 to 191, mean 190.4 (120 pairs) |
| Best board, strict 5/5, border still fully matched | yes and yes (440, 40 breaks) |

Every one of the sixteen borders lands inside a six-point window, 434 to 440,
and the completions differ almost everywhere: between any two, 188 to 191 of
the 196 interior cells hold a different piece (191 is the maximum possible,
since the five clues sit on interior cells and always agree). The best board
of the run, a legal strict-5/5 completion of a fully-matched border, is
viewable from the committed results file in the reproduction topic.

### The second seed base

A second, independent pass (twelve borders, a different seed base) agrees:
band 434 to 441 (median 438), best 441, interior tile-Hamming 187 to 191 (mean
190.3), and the best board again a strict-5/5 completion of a fully-matched
border. Across 28 distinct borders, not one crosses 445.

### The control: one border, many seeds

A narrow band across borders only means something next to the spread for a
single border. The control completes each of 16 borders with 8 different beam
seeds and keeps every result (128 completions, no best-of):

| Quantity | Result |
| --- | --- |
| All 128 completions | 432 to 442 |
| Per-border mean score | 434.5 to 437.4 (grand mean 436.0) |
| Mean square between borders / within a border | 5.88 / 4.75 |
| One-way ANOVA, border effect on score | F(15, 112) = 1.24, p = 0.25 |
| Share of score variance carried by the border (ICC) | about 3% |
| Interior Hamming, same border, different seed | mean 184.1 (min 166, 448 pairs) |
| Interior Hamming, different borders | mean 190.4 (min 185, 7,680 pairs) |

Two readings follow. The border does not detectably predict the score: the
spread between borders is the size the seed noise alone would produce. And the
near-total difference between tops is mostly not the border's doing either:
two seeds on the *same* border already disagree on about 184 of the 196
interior cells, against about 190 for two different borders. Most of what
looked like border-driven diversity is the producer's own seed sensitivity.

## Why a stronger interior matters

An earlier notebook pass ran this test with a weaker, hard-fit interior that
stalled in the 250s. This reproduction swaps in the break-tolerant beam above,
which always fills all 196 cells and lands in the mid-430s. The negative holds
for both producers, but both end far below the record, and a producer whose
ceiling is the same for every border cannot reveal a border effect that only
shows up near the top. The result is therefore a property of these producers
and this budget, not yet of the frame-to-interior coupling in general.

## What this shows, and what it does not

With these producers, a different fully-matched border gives a different
board but not a measurably better one, so border-first diversification bought
nothing here. Where the high-scoring information actually lives is plausibly
the interior, the region the [rigidity wall](/research/why/rigidity-wall)
describes as a frozen island, but this experiment does not prove that. The seam
bookkeeping that couples the border to the interior is the subject of the
[border-balance page](/research/why/border-balance).

The open control is the informative one: freeze the border of a near-record
board and complete it with a producer that can reach that band, then compare
with random borders under the same producer. That needs a board outside the
public starter kit and a much stronger interior producer, and it is not run
here. Until it is, the negative is scoped to producers that top out near 440.
