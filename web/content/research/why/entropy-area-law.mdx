Forget the use-each-piece-once rule for a moment and treat the 196 interior
pieces as reusable tiles. Count the valid all-matched patches and they grow
exponentially with size: there's no shortage of locally-valid ways to tile.
The matching grammar is rich, not restrictive.

We can measure that richness exactly. For a strip of width $n$, the growth
rate per cell is an entropy density $h(n)$, and the sequence decreases toward
the true two-dimensional value as the strip widens.

## Then the use-once rule bites

Now put the use-each-piece-once rule back, and ask how often a color-valid
patch actually uses distinct pieces. Call it $\rho$. For square blocks we
count it exactly, in-repo: for each interior block size $n$ we count $A(n)$,
the color-valid fillings when pieces may repeat, and $B(n)$, those that use
distinct pieces, and take $\rho(n) = B(n)/A(n)$. That gives 0.892 at 2×2 and
0.648 at 3×3, with $B(2) = 4{,}059{,}952$ matching the in-repo sub-grid
reference table. At $n=4$ the reusable count $A(4)$ is already
$6.3\times10^{16}$, past exact distinct enumeration in reasonable time.
Beyond that we sample: drawing color-valid patches four cells wide uniformly
at random (exactly, through their completion counts) gives $\rho = 0.33$ at
16 cells, 0.11 at 24, 0.024 at 32 and 0.0004 at 48. Two runs with different
seeds give 0.3263 and 0.3261, 0.1060 and 0.1071, 0.0238 and 0.0244, 0.000375
and 0.000389; they agree within their combined standard error (the largest
gap, at 32 cells, is 1.8 combined standard errors).

It collapses, and faster than an area law: $-\ln \rho$ per cell rises from
0.07 at 16 cells to 0.16 at 48. Two effects add. Side-by-side cells hold the
same piece about 2.6% of the time in a color-valid patch, five times the
chance level of 1 in 196, and any two cells of the patch can hold the same
piece, so the number of chances for a repeat grows with the square of the
patch size. The fraction of realizable patches drops below one in a thousand
near 44 cells (44.2 and 44.4 in the two runs, by log-linear interpolation
between 32 and 48 cells). That figure rests on sampled values, not on exact
counts. An earlier version of this page fitted
$\rho(n) \approx \exp(-0.044\, n^2)$ to the two exact points and
extrapolated a collapse near 155 cells; the sampled fraction at 48 cells is
about 300 times below that fit, so the fit and the 155-cell figure are
withdrawn.

## Why it matters

The matching grammar stays rich, then the distinctness rule removes a fraction
of the valid patches that grows with their area. That is the measured
statement, and it is narrower than "all the hardness lives in the use-once
rule". The counts here are for small patches with free boundaries. They do
not show that edge matching alone is easy for the real task, a 16×16 region
with a fixed grey frame and global closure: bounded tiling with reusable tiles
and a fixed boundary is already NP-complete
([van Emde Boas, 1997](https://doi.org/10.1201/9780429187490-12)), and so are
edge-matching puzzles in general
([Demaine and Demaine, 2007](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00373-007-0713-4)).
Whether the reusable-tile 16×16 instance with a grey frame is easy in
practice has not been measured.

See [interpolating between record boards fails](/research/why/sigma-cycles).

## The theorem, briefly

The per-width entropy has a well-defined limit. Joining an $n_1$-wide and an
$n_2$-wide strip side by side only adds a seam constraint, so the eigenvalues
satisfy

$$
\lambda_{n_1+n_2} \;\le\; \lambda_{n_1}\,\lambda_{n_2}.
$$

Taking logs makes $\log \lambda_n$ subadditive, and Fekete's lemma gives the
limit as an infimum, so every computed width bounds it from above:

$$
h_\infty \;=\; \lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log_{10}\lambda_n}{n} \;=\; \inf_n \frac{\log_{10}\lambda_n}{n}.
$$

The upper bound is the purely horizontal rate: ignoring vertical constraints
only adds patches, so

$$
0 \;\le\; h_\infty \;\le\; \log_{10}\lambda_H \;=\; 1.6645,
$$

with $\lambda_H = 46.18$ the spectral radius of the horizontal
color-compatibility matrix. Because the limit is an infimum, every computed
width is an upper bound: $h(6) = 0.640$ gives $h_\infty \le 0.640$. The
increments $\log_{10}(\lambda_{n+1}/\lambda_n)$ sit at 0.4352 from width 2 to
width 6, so the limit is close to 0.435, next to the mean-field value
$\log_{10}(784/289) = 0.433$. Strict positivity does not follow from this
argument: many one-dimensional chains do not by themselves give
two-dimensional entropy.

What the theorem proves is that the limit exists and lies below every strip
value. Its value, about 0.435, is read from the strip eigenvalues for widths
1 to 6 (1.6645, 1.0497, 0.8449, 0.7425, 0.6810, 0.6400), recomputed by the
[topic's scripts](https://github.com/raphael-anjou/eternity2/tree/main/research/topics/entropy-area-law/compute/scripts).
An earlier version of this page gave the limit as about 0.67; since $h(6)$ is
already below that, the figure is impossible and is withdrawn. The
distinct-piece fractions beyond 3×3 are sampled, not counted, and the theorem
does not fix them.
