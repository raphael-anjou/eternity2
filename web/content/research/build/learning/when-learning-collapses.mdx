The other four pages in this section are about what learning from strong boards
*buys*. This one is about where it stops, and it is the page that keeps the family
grounded, because every technique here showed the same two failure modes in these
runs, and a reader should not mistake a bag of learned tricks for a way past the
plateau. All of it comes from exploratory runs on a private corpus (boards
committed and verifiable, searches not re-runnable here), with a few seeds each
and no baseline at equal compute.

## Failure one: the signal becomes the objective

In these experiments a learned signal behaved best as a *tiebreak*: it decides
between moves that are otherwise equal on the one thing that actually counts, the
matched edge in front of you. Promoted to a large, fixed share of the objective,
it makes the search trade a real matched edge against a statistical hunch, and in
the one case measured, the search collapsed.

[LODESTONE](/research/lab/experiments/raphael-anjou/learning/lodestone) is that
case. Across five seeds, a tiny weight on its scarce-piece-early prior moved the
median from-scratch score from 449 to 451 (inside the base spread of 446 to 451)
and tightened the spread to 450 to 451; higher fixed weights gave 422, then 380.
The same shape appears in [anti-pattern mining](/research/build/learning/anti-pattern-mining)
from the other direction: the run that used the trap list as a steer reached 463,
but hard-*banning* trapped placements made boards worse. The safe reading is
narrow: in this beam, at fixed weights, the prior had to stay small relative to
matched edges. Annealed or adaptive weights were not tried, and
[KEYRING](/research/lab/experiments/raphael-anjou/learning/keyring) mixed three
signals into its score and still reached 460, so this is a tuning result, not a
law of the family.

## Failure two: the plateau is not the puzzle's

The deeper limit is quieter, because it looks like success. Used as a tiebreak, a
learned signal carried each search to the top of *its own range* and no further.
LODESTONE improved consistency by a couple of edges and left its range's top where
it was. [PRIOR](/research/lab/experiments/raphael-anjou/learning/prior)
and [KEYRING](/research/lab/experiments/raphael-anjou/learning/keyring) reach the
mid-to-high 450s from an empty grid and 460 after a local-refinement tail.
[PALIMPSEST](/research/lab/experiments/raphael-anjou/learning/palimpsest), the strongest
of them, reaches 463, and rebuilding its trapped regions lands back on the same
known top board rather than a new one.

That plateau is a measured limit of these runs, not a property of the puzzle:
community boards already sit above it ([records](/research/records)). We have not
shown why these methods stop where they do. The candidates are the
[rigidity wall](/research/why/rigidity-wall) (near a strong board the remaining
mismatches lock into an interlocking [σ-cycle](/research/why/sigma-cycles) that the
local moves tried here did not open), the methods themselves, and the filter that
chose the corpus. One tempting argument does not hold: that a signal mined from
sub-perfect boards cannot lead past them. Learning from sub-optimal data plus
search routinely exceeds the data when the learner re-learns from its own improved
samples, as the [cross-entropy method](https://doi.org/10.1023/A:1010091220143) and
estimation-of-distribution algorithms do. None of the experiments here re-mined
its own outputs.

## What the family is actually for

None of this makes learning from strong boards worthless. It makes it *precise*. A
learned signal was, in these runs, one way to get a search *to* the plateau: to
build a competitive board from nothing (PRIOR, KEYRING), to reach it in a fresh
basin (KEYRING's new corner family, credited to its signals together with injected
randomness, which no ablation separates), to land at the top of the range more
consistently (LODESTONE's tightened spread over five seeds), or to diagnose a move
a search could not represent
([REPLAY](/research/lab/experiments/raphael-anjou/learning/replay)'s double break).

Getting *past* the plateau is open, not ruled out. The construct-then-refine
pipelines that reach this project's best boards divide the labour: a learned
constructor to reach the top of the range, then a separate refinement. A learner
that iterates on its own improved boards is the obvious next experiment.
