This is the board of open angles: one row per direction still worth a run.
Each entry states the question in a line, names the wall it attacks (from
[why it's hard](/research/why)), points to what has already been tried and
where it stopped (in the [wall-and-method map](/research/why/walls-and-methods)
and the [dead ends](/research/build/dead-ends)), and tags how approachable it
is. The tags mean what they say: **newcomer-friendly** is a target you can pick
up without a decade of context; **hard, well-mapped** is a target that many
strong attempts have charted and none has crossed; **unattended** is an angle
nobody is actively working, distinct from one that has been attacked and held.

For the state of the art the score lives on the [records](/research/records)
page; this page is only the part that is still open.

The [full-solve series](/research/lab/experiments/raphael-anjou/full-solve) prices a complete search of the real pieces and
settles several questions that used to sit on this frontier. They are marked
below as answered or priced, and the questions the series leaves open follow
them, each with what would settle it and a rough cost.

## The 470-to-480 gap

<Callout kind="open" title="Can any method cross from the 470 ceiling to a full 480?">
  The canonical track. The best board respecting only the mandatory starter
  clue has stood at 470/480 since the community reached it, and a designed 480
  is a given: the publisher built the puzzle from one solution, so a perfect
  board exists and only its location is open.
</Callout>

**Wall.** [Rigidity](/research/why/rigidity-wall): the record boards are
locally frozen islands, and the ten missing edges are on the far side of every
[structural wall at once](/research/why/walls-and-methods).

**Tried, and where it stopped.** Every method in the
[wall-and-method map](/research/why/walls-and-methods) ends against rigidity;
the from-scratch and corpus builds top out at 458 to 463, and none crosses to
the ceiling. Raw compute alone does not close it either, which is recorded
under [just throw more compute at it](/research/build/dead-ends).

**Entry tag.** Hard, well-mapped. This is the summit of the whole puzzle: a
great deal is known about why it resists, which is exactly why a fresh crossing
would be a headline.

## The strict five-clue target, one edge at a time

<Callout kind="open" title="Can a board that honours all five official clues beat the strict record?">
  The strict five-clue line is tracked separately from the open 470 line,
  because [most progress on the open record was made without the clues](/research/build/clue-puzzles).
  The best board respecting all five placements is kept on [the records page](/research/records).
</Callout>

**Wall.** [Rigidity](/research/why/rigidity-wall) again, plus the clue
placements narrow the reachable region: fewer boards satisfy all five, so the
haystack is smaller but so is the needle count.

**Tried, and where it stopped.** The strict line moved three times in 2026 (the
dates and boards are on [the records page](/research/records)); the community's
[distributed fleet](/research/community/five-clue-fleet) is still running and its
stated target is exactly this one. The project's own
strict builds and re-squeezes reach the 460 class but not past it, as charted in
the [wall-and-method map](/research/why/walls-and-methods).

**Entry tag.** Hard, well-mapped, but a smaller step than the 470-to-480 gap:
the next strict mark is a single edge away, on a line where the reachable region is tighter
and therefore easier to reason about than the open record. It is also the one
open problem on this page with a running, joinable search behind it.

## Brendan Owen's hint-free 10x10 set_2

<Callout kind="open" title="Solve set_2, the hint-free 10x10 that has never been solved.">
  Brendan Owen published a pair of generated hint-free 10x10 puzzles with their
  statistics. One (set_1) fell; the other (set_2) has never been solved and
  remains the community's agreed next rung below the full 16x16.
</Callout>

**Wall.** The same edge-matching hardness as the full puzzle, at a size where
solvers can find a solution in practice. Set_1's first solution came after
about 2×10¹⁷ nodes (about 180 core-years in 2017; a first hit, not an
exhaustive search), and a first solution of set_2 should cost the same order, so it is
a search-engineering target, not a structural one. One solve is a random draw:
on set_1's statistics, the waiting time to the first solution spans about a
twentieth to three times the expected cost (5th to 95th percentile).

**Tried, and where it stopped.** The full story is on the
[benchmarks](/research/build/benchmarks#brendans-1010s-one-fell-one-is-still-standing)
page: set_1 was solved, set_2's statistics are known, and it is
[still standing](/research/build/benchmarks#brendans-1010s-one-fell-one-is-still-standing).

**Entry tag.** Hard, well-priced. Its cost is known in advance, and it is sized
for a long distributed run or a much faster engine, not a weekend on one PC. For
a first attempt, climb the 7×7 to 9×9
[benchmark ladder](/research/build/benchmarks#what-to-run-today) first.

## Crossing a rigidity basin

<Callout kind="open" title="Is there any local path from one record basin to another?">
  The record basins tested so far (three to four, including a 469) are measured
  locally optimal by MIP out to a multi-cell halo (runs not committed); whether every record board is
  equally rigid is untested. The open question is whether any sequence of small,
  legal moves reaches a different basin at all.
</Callout>

**Wall.** [Rigidity](/research/why/rigidity-wall), stated in its sharpest form,
and [interpolating between record boards fails](/research/why/sigma-cycles): the
loop from one great board to another is one giant swap, and every partial step
along it scores worse than its start.

**Tried, and where it stopped.** The rigidity page records MIP
halo-optimality certificates across several basins (notebook runs, not
committed), and the search for a short escape chain
between basins failed for a structural reason set out on
[interpolating between record boards fails](/research/why/sigma-cycles). The
[recombine two good boards](/research/build/dead-ends) entry is the same wall
from the crossover side.

**Entry tag.** Hard, well-mapped. A single crossing move, or a proof that none
exists, would reshape how every local-search method is judged.

## The meet-in-the-middle memory wall

<Callout kind="open" title="Can the two halves of a meet-in-the-middle search be made to meet at full size?">
  Meeting two partial searches in the middle is complete in principle, but the
  number of partial boards to store grows about twentyfold per extra allowed
  mismatch, so the halves stop meeting well before the full 16x16.
</Callout>

**Wall.** The exactness cost measured directly: the
[meet-in-the-middle](/research/build/exact/meet-in-the-middle) approach is
exact but its memory grows past what any machine holds.

**Tried, and where it stopped.** BANDSAW solved an endgame band to proven
optimality and in doing so measured the roughly twentyfold-per-mismatch growth
on both sides, so meeting in the middle stops paying at full size; that result
is the [BANDSAW row](/research/why/walls-and-methods) of the map. A better
representation, or a lossy meeting that stays sound, is the open lever.

**Entry tag.** Hard, well-mapped. The wall here is quantified, so progress is
measurable: any encoding that lowers the per-mismatch growth factor is a real
gain even short of a full solve.

## A diversity axis beyond parallel tempering

<Callout kind="open" title="Is there a search mechanism that reliably finds new basins, beyond parallel tempering?">
  Almost every method keeps rediscovering the same handful of basins. In a full
  method sweep, only parallel tempering reliably produced genuinely new ones,
  and even it plateaus quickly.
</Callout>

**Wall.** [Rigidity](/research/why/rigidity-wall) seen as a diversity
bottleneck: the constraint is not the score a method reaches but the number of
distinct basins it can find, and the standard arsenal finds too few.

**Tried, and where it stopped.** The diversity survey is written up on the
[wall-and-method map](/research/why/walls-and-methods): adaptive local search,
snake placement, and the top of a trained generator's distribution all keep
landing in the known basins, and only parallel tempering reliably escapes.
[Generate boards with a transformer](/research/build/dead-ends) is one of the
diversity attempts that did not hold.

**Entry tag.** Unattended, and open-ended. Unlike the mapped targets above,
this one has no charted attempt list waiting to be beaten: a genuinely new
diversity mechanism is an idea nobody has planted yet, which makes it the most
speculative entry here and the one least constrained by prior work.

## Does the distinct-piece collapse bear on local rigidity?

<Callout kind="open" title="Is there any link between the record boards freezing and the collapse of distinct-piece patches?">
  This question was first asked because the two seemed to share a scale, a
  patch of low hundreds of cells. They do not. Among colour-valid patches
  four cells wide, the share that uses distinct pieces falls below one in a
  thousand near 44 cells, not 155 as first extrapolated. The loops that
  separate the best known boards have a median of 119 cells, the same as
  between two random reshuffles of the pieces. What remains open is whether
  the collapse, measured on small free-boundary patches, says anything about
  rigidity at all.
</Callout>

**Wall.** [Rigidity](/research/why/rigidity-wall) and
[the distinct-piece fraction](/research/why/entropy-area-law). Rigidity is
measured independently, on the boards tested, by MIP halo optimality runs
and a committed SAT halo; the distinct-piece fraction is counted exactly up
to 3×3 and sampled up to 48 cells. A reduction of one to the other would tie
the two together, but nothing on-site derives it.

**Tried, and where it stopped.** The coincidence of scale that motivated the
question was checked and withdrawn: the collapse point came from a fit that
the sampled fractions refute, and the loop sizes between record boards match
a random null ([interpolating between record boards fails](/research/why/sigma-cycles)).
The rigidity result is region by region and stands without invoking the
collapse. No page asserts a causal bridge, and no measurement now suggests
one.

**Entry tag.** Unattended, premise withdrawn. Kept so that the withdrawn
coincidence is not rediscovered as a lead; a link would need a mechanism
that works on boards with a fixed frame, not a shared length scale.

## Does the hardness peak produce the no-forced-moves branching profile?

<Callout kind="open" title="Is the high branching factor forced by sitting near the hardness peak?">
  Every interior cell keeps 73 to 137 legal pieces, and the interior colour
  count (17) sits near the ensemble hardness band where Owen's first-moment
  estimate gives about one solution (the real piece set is estimated at about
  14,702 with the starter). Both are grouped as "nothing
  local to prune," but no derivation ties the branching count to sitting at
  roughly 17 interior colours.
</Callout>

**Wall.** [No forced moves](/research/why/no-forced-moves) and
[the hardness peak](/research/why/phase-transition). The branching count is a
per-cell candidate count on the piece set; the peak is a statement about the
expected number of solutions of random puzzles near 17 colours. A puzzle could in principle have one without
the other.

**Tried, and where it stopped.** [Prune versus speed](/research/why/prune-vs-speed)
and the [wall-and-method map](/research/why/walls-and-methods) group both walls
under the same theme, that there is nothing local to prune, which is a thematic
union rather than a causal claim. No page derives the branching profile from the
colour count.

**Entry tag.** Hard, well-mapped. The two walls are each measured exactly; the
missing piece is the derivation connecting them, which would turn a shared theme
into a mechanism.

## Does the sigma-cycle geometry cap constructive search, not just local repair?

<Callout kind="open" title="Is the sigma-cycle wall the reason beam and DFS producers also saturate, or only local search?">
  The move between two record boards is one indivisible cycle of many cells, and
  every proper prefix scores worse; that shows why local repair cannot cross
  between basins. The open question is whether the same geometry is what caps
  constructive DFS and beam producers, which saturate at 458 to 463 for reasons
  computed only between finished boards, not inside the search tree.
</Callout>

**Wall.** [Sigma-cycles](/research/why/sigma-cycles) and
[rigidity](/research/why/rigidity-wall), seen on the diversity axis. The
sigma-cycle result is an exact statement over every bundled board pair, but it
is computed between finished record boards, not about the constructive search
tree that produces them.

**Tried, and where it stopped.** The [wall-and-method map](/research/why/walls-and-methods)
documents that from-scratch and beam producers saturate like the others, and the
diversity survey found the standard arsenal keeps rediscovering the same basins.
Extending the sigma-cycle mechanism to explain that constructive ceiling, rather
than only the local-repair one, is unproven and asserted nowhere on-site.

**Entry tag.** Hard, well-mapped, and close to the diversity question above: a
proof that the same cycle geometry governs constructive saturation would unify
two independently observed ceilings.

## What the series leaves open

Each item below is stated as the series leaves it. None is a record claim, and
the toy-board items are not Eternity II progress.

### Frames away from the clue corners

**Question.** Can a wrong frame whose cheap refutation does not come from a
clue corner be refuted for less than the search it saves?
**Status.** Undecided. On the real pieces the far frames have a median of 75.12
bits with an interval of [42.64, inf] (16 of 37 censored), and an extra far set
left 29 of 52 censored; part of that censoring is a counting failure, not a
refuter failure. The overall median passes a necessary condition only.
**What would settle it.** Refutation costs on far frames with the censoring
resolved, or a bound showing they sit above break-even. **Cost.** Not costed;
the extra far set alone took 1.1 hours at a 600 s cap. A pass would reopen frame
search driven by learned cores
([frame and corners](/research/lab/experiments/raphael-anjou/full-solve/frame-and-corners)).

### The three annealed fill orders

**Question.** Is any of the three annealed static orders cheaper than row scan?
**Status.** Undecided in both directions. The estimator's population dies at
free depth 38 to 48 on those orders, against 191 to 198 for row scan, so the
estimates are lower bounds on the tree, not sizes. **What would settle it.** An
estimator that survives to depth on those orders, or an exact count on a
smaller reproduction. **Cost.** Not costed. A certificate that row scan is the
best static order has not been written either; it would be a proof, not a gain
([approaches priced out](/research/lab/experiments/raphael-anjou/full-solve/approaches-priced-out)).

### Exactness of the convex lift at 6x6

**Question.** Does the degree-2 lift with nonnegativity stay exact (no weight
on placements that no solution uses) at 6x6? **Status.** No reading. Three
largest-leak runs ended unconverged, with 0.73 to 0.87 of the mass per cell off
solutions and flat, which decides nothing. At 5x5, seed 0 is undecided and
seed 3 leaks (one solver family only). **What would settle it.** A converged
largest-leak solve on the three 6x6 boards, and a second solver family on
seed 3. **Cost.** 6 to 170 core-hours for the first (a lower bound of 13 to 24
hours per board was seen), about 20 core-minutes for the second. Even if
exactness held, that alone is worth 0 bits; it would justify 7x7 and 8x8 tests
([relaxations on small boards](/research/lab/experiments/raphael-anjou/full-solve/relaxations-on-small-boards)).

### Clause learning against propagation on real-piece subtrees

**Question.** Does the lead of a learning solver over a propagation-only control
rise with depth and with size? **Status.** Measured, negative,
independently re-checked. On 160 wrong prefixes of the real pieces at depths 112
to 88 the pre-stated stop line fired: clause learning gives no lead in search
work over row scan with arc consistency (at most -0.25 bits at depth 88). The
toy-board size clause leans fired but is not established, and figures in seconds
are properties of the programs run. See [learning against
propagation](/research/lab/experiments/raphael-anjou/full-solve/learning-against-propagation).
**What would settle the rest.** An 8x8 rung and a learner with short
explanations built on a propagator; neither was run.

### Growth of non-tree solvers against the tree

**Question.** On the same boards, does the work of annealing, a difference map,
a low-rank form or message passing grow more slowly with size than the tree?
**Status.** Partial. The low-rank form needs 62 times more evaluations per
solution from 5x5 to 6x6 (exponent 4.2 in the number of placements, 3.4 to 5.1);
a larger batch gives 4.54, not independently checked. Two sizes cannot separate
a power from an exponential. The difference-map bands solved 0 of 30 at 7x7.
**What would settle it.** Work-to-solution on 5x5 to 7x7 for each arm, against
the tree on the same boards. **Cost.** About 120 core-hours. The audit that
listed it puts one arm surviving at 5 percent and a gain of 10 bits at 0.5 to 1
percent (guesses, not independently checked).

## When to reopen the closed routes

The pricing keeps one rule: reopen a route only for a price of 10 bits or more
and a falsifying experiment that takes under a day. The conditions that would
justify it ([approach pricing](https://github.com/raphael-anjou/eternity2/tree/main/research/experiments/approach-pricing)):

1. A published method shows a class-level gain. Price it against the routes
   index and the ceiling table first: a check that only bites after depth 128
   cannot pass ([late filters](/research/lab/experiments/raphael-anjou/full-solve/late-filters)).
2. Tiles of the solution become available from outside the search. A pinned
   tile is worth about 10 bits at best and about 6 at the median on painted
   boards, and a run of about 10^15 nodes would need more than 10 spread pins,
   probably 16 to 24 ([worth of a known tile](/research/lab/experiments/raphael-anjou/full-solve/worth-of-a-known-tile)).
3. Hardware improves by 15 orders of magnitude or more. Every credible
   multiplier together is about 2.5 orders.
4. A run or a re-check overturns a closure: the convex lift is exact at 6x6; the
   far frames resolve below break-even; a non-tree solver's work grows by less
   than 0.75 of the tree's from 6x6 to 7x7; a learning solver's lead over
   propagation alone rises with size.

Two smaller items are proofs rather than gains: a second reading of the 2-bit
bound on a sound sketch, and the certificate for row scan above.
