How faithful is our port of the Blackwood record backtracker, and does it matter?
This is the record regime (a stochastic hunt for high matched-edge boards), not
exact search. The port is the engine behind the Blackwood-style row of the
[single-core benchmark](/research/lab/experiments/single-core-benchmark), and
several later experiments are copies of it. Every check was made against the
published C# source (commit f544e42), read for this purpose, not against the
author's results. The engine itself was not run: its random generator makes a
node-for-node comparison impossible, so fidelity here means agreement with the
source by reading, with tests, and with the earlier port by replay.

## Seven differences

| # | Difference | Status |
|--:|:--|:--|
| 6 | The no-break path could place a piece showing grey toward an interior cell | fixed, always on |
| 2 | The break path placed non-physical pieces, ignored the ring rule, tried privileged-first | fixed |
| 3 | An isolation rule for breaks that the published engine does not have | fixed, off by default |
| 1 | Colour keying and quota reading (a count silently changed meaning with the keying) | fixed |
| 4 | Centre piece counted from depth 0, with no node or quota test | fixed |
| 7 | Quota tested per node at every depth, not per candidate up to depth 160 | fixed |
| 5 | Scan order (rows from the top-left, not the published board order) | fixed |

Each fix has a unit test, and six of the seven have a legacy switch that restores
the earlier behaviour (item 6 has none). The first list of differences missed
item 7 and described items 4 and 5 wrongly: the published scan order does not
interleave the border, and the centre piece sits where the published engine puts
it (the real difference was when it is counted).

The orientation defect (item 6) mattered most. In 5 of 31 comparison runs it
stranded the search in an illegal subtree (2 to 8 legal nodes in 10^8 to 1.3 x
10^8 nodes before the fix). It also inflated runs: on no-clue seed 106 the old
binary reached depth 240 through illegal placements, where the fixed engine
reaches 231, with 24.3 million of its 10^8 nodes in illegal subtrees. Removing it
changes only illegal subtrees: in the 26 runs it did not strand, the per-depth
legal-node histogram is identical with and without the fix.

## How it was checked

Independently re-checked twice, by two analyses that read the published source
separately from the author's notes.

| Check | Result |
|:--|:--|
| Unit tests at the final commit | 13 of 13 pass |
| Mutation sweep (revert one fix at a time) | 10 of 11 reverts caught at first; the survivor (break-candidate order) now has a test that fails when it is reverted |
| Legacy switches against the earlier port, 10^8 nodes, no-clue 101 to 104 and 108, five-clue 501 to 506 | 11 of 11 identical (nodes, depth, scores, board) |
| New defaults against the first re-check's binary, 10^7 nodes, per-depth histograms | 12 of 12 identical |
| Piece and colour mapping to the published list | all 256 pieces match under one colour bijection; centre piece and rotation match |
| Published scan-order table | identical entry for entry |

Together, the two replays give 23 of 23 node-sequence comparisons identical. In the
first re-check, 13 of 16 legacy replays matched; the other three were exactly the
item 6 runs, reproduced by the frozen older engine with and without its fix.

Speed is a cost of the fix, not a gain. The no-break path now takes about 15 to 19
percent more time per node than the earlier port (user seconds per 10^8 nodes:
1.05 before, 1.24 to 1.28 after in the first timing; ratios 1.15 to 1.19 on the loaded
host in the second). The cause was not located.

## With five clues, the faithful defaults do worse

The published engine places only the centre piece. Its rules with all five clues
are therefore this port's extrapolation, not something the source settles. On the
official pieces with five clues, one restart and 10^8 nodes per seed, old defaults
(a) against the faithful defaults (c):

| seed | old: deepest | old: legal nodes past depth 200 | old: best | faithful: deepest | faithful: legal nodes past depth 200 | faithful: best |
|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| 501 | 215 | 1,271 | 443 | 54 | 0 | 372 |
| 502 | 200 | 0 | 431 | 18 | 0 | 369 |
| 503 | 33 | 0 | 381 | 18 | 0 | 382 |
| 504 | 199 | 0 | none | 18 | 0 | none |
| 505 | 197 | 0 | 425 | 18 | 0 | 373 |
| 506 | 205 | 258 | 425 | 18 | 0 | 377 |
| 507 | 197 | 0 | 425 | 18 | 0 | 361 |
| 508 | 33 | 0 | none | 33 | 0 | 378 |

"Best" is the matched-edges score of a greedy completion of the partial board;
no run completed a board by search, and "none" means no completion was emitted.
The orientation defect strands the old engine on seed 508 (8 legal nodes), so that
row is not like for like. Over the eight seeds the
old defaults place 1,529 legal nodes past depth 200, all on seeds 501 and 506;
the faithful defaults place none. On six of eight seeds the faithful defaults
never get past depth 18, the cell directly below a clue that the published scan
order reaches only at depth 34. The wall also appears on seeds 1 to 4 at 10^7
nodes. No single legacy switch restores the old depths; the published order
meeting a clue's neighbour sixteen cells early combines with the keying, quota
and clue counting. Eight seeds support ranges, not a ratio, and the host was
loaded.

## What it means

The faithful defaults are for the centre-clue and no-clue settings, where 11 of 12
default runs reached depth 244 to 247 within 10^8 nodes. For five clues, use the legacy profile
until a five-clue schedule is designed and measured. For exact search nothing
changes: a port's fidelity cannot move the size of the tree. Its cost with five
clues, and the gap against what is affordable, are priced on the
[series hub](/research/lab/experiments/raphael-anjou/full-solve); this page adds
nothing to those bits, and a port that is more faithful but searches shallower
under five clues is a step away from them. Record scores are on
[/research/records](/research/records), not restated here.

Two earlier statements need care. The benchmark's committed Blackwood-style row
(mean 436.4, best 440, matched-edges convention) came from the old defaults, so
it measures the earlier port, not this one. And the benchmark page's finding that
the community's privileged colours do not transfer compared three interior
colours, which is not the published set; in this repository's numbering the
published set is {5, 20, 7}. That comparison does not test the claim it was
cited for.

## What was not run

The published C# engine; multi-threaded runs; clippy and formatting; a quiet-host
absolute timing; the first-corner rule (the published engine fixes its first
corner and never backtracks into it, and the port does not implement this); the
break path's wall-clock cost. The port's node unit (one per cell entry; his is
one per loop iteration) and its restart and randomisation scheme still differ, so
node caps and rates are not comparable without conversion.

## Reproduce

Build the workspace with `cargo build --release --manifest-path engine/Cargo.toml`
in the experiment folder. The commands, raw outputs and comparison scripts are
under `engine/crates/bench-audit/fidelity-results/`; the written comparison with
its per-item evidence is `engine/crates/bench-audit/src/bin/FIDELITY.md`. See the
[dead ends](/research/build/dead-ends) and the
[open problems](/research/open-problems) for where this sits.
