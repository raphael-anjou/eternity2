## The question and the answer

What would an exact solution of the 16x16 cost by search, and can any known family of ideas close the gap between that cost and what can be afforded? Answer: no route examined closes it. The largest gain that survived an independent re-check is about 5.5 bits of nodes, against a gap of 56 to 73 bits (budgets of 2^78 and 2^61.5 nodes, table below). The work is parked, not proven impossible: several closures rest on one reader or one solver family, and the page marks which. The conditions that would reopen it are listed below.

The 84 bits belongs to the 10^15 run and the 56 to 73 bits to the two larger budgets; the two are never combined. A tree size is an estimate from a particle sampler (one million particles), not an enumeration; the measured values differ from the random-model figures by -0.033, +0.0004, +0.009 and +0.007 digits (all within 0.04; the model figures are carried over from the earlier pricing, not recomputed here).

## The one-sentence reason

Every check that works, works too late. The tests that refute wrong boards refute nearly all of them only from depth 144 to 192, while the widest level of the measured tree is at depth 158, and a perfect free cut at depth 144 would save at most 2.4 bits of 134.4. The argument and its ceiling table are on [late filters](/research/lab/experiments/raphael-anjou/full-solve/late-filters).

## What would reopen this

From the tripwires in the approach-pricing record. Any one of them is enough.

1. A published method shows a class-level gain. Price it against the routes index and the ceiling table first.
2. Tiles of the solution become available from outside the search. An extra pin is worth about 10 bits at best and about 6 at the median on painted boards, and a 10^15 run needs more than 10, probably 16 to 24, spread pins.
3. Hardware improves by 15 orders of magnitude or more. Every credible multiplier together is about 2.5 orders.
4. A re-check or a run overturns a closure: the convex lift turns out exact at 6x6; the far stratum of frame cores resolves below break-even; a non-tree solver's work grows by less than 0.75 of the tree's from 6x6 to 7x7; a learning solver's lead over propagation alone rises with size.

The standing rule for reopening is a price of 10 bits or more and a falsifying experiment that takes under a day.
