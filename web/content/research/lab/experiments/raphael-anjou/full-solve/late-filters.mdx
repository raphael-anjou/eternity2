Can a cheap check, added to the row scan, cut enough of the tree to matter? The
answer depends on one number per check: the depth at which it starts to remove
wrong boards. Nothing a filter does before that depth counts, and the tree is
thin there. The checks tried bite late, so the answer is no.

## The setup

The exact row-scan tree on the real pieces with all five clues holds 10^40.457
nodes, or 2^134.4 (95% interval 10^40.440 to 10^40.473, five repetitions at a
million particles each; see [the measured size](/research/lab/experiments/raphael-anjou/full-solve/price-of-a-full-search)).
Its widest level sits at free depth 158 (free depth counts placed non-clue
cells). About 10^15 nodes are affordable for a modest run, so the gap is about
84 bits; against the larger efforts priced on the
[series hub](/research/lab/experiments/raphael-anjou/full-solve) it is 56 to 73
bits.

Take a filter that removes every wrong node from free depth d onward, for free
and with no false cuts. It cannot do better than the share of the tree that sits
at depth d or deeper. The saving in bits is log2 of the total over the nodes
above that depth.

## The ceiling of a perfect free cut

| Free depth | 52 | 64 | 80 | 96 | 112 | 128 | 144 | 158 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Most it can save (bits) | 57.1 | 44.8 | 29.8 | 17.1 | 10.7 | 6.8 | 2.4 | 0.5 |

**Status: derived arithmetic from the measured level profile, recomputed by a
second analysis. It is not an experiment.** The profile is measured and
independently checked; the table is a sum over it, with no run of its own. It
bounds the gross gain of any filter that removes nothing before depth d. It
ignores what the filter costs, so a real gain is smaller still. A filter that
also acts earlier is bounded by the ceiling at its earliest depth, not its
latest.

## Where the tests bite

Two measurements on generated 16x16 boards with the official colour census
(toy boards, never Eternity II progress) put the checks on that axis.

**Arc consistency.** Run alone, with no search, on prefixes sampled from the row
scan at each depth. This follow-up was **not independently checked**, and the
engine was changed after the experiment it extends was judged. It covers 15
boards, four scan directions each (60 trees).

| Free depth | 96 | 112 | 128 | 144 | 152 | 160 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sampled wrong prefixes still consistent | 0.427 | 0.096 | 0.179 | 0.064 | 0.007 | 0 |
| Refuted (percent, derived) | 57 | 90 | 82 | 94 | 99 | 100 |

It never refuted the true prefix. The coarser observation in the main
experiment, which was independently reproduced, covers 192 board-direction rows:
arc consistency refutes every sampled prefix in 189 of them at depth 160 and in
all 192 at depth 176. The share unrefuted counts sampled prefixes, not work, so it
is not the saving. The saving priced for arc consistency is about 5.5 bits of
nodes, while one check costs about 13 bits per node, so it does not pay.

**Colour census.** A statistic of the multiset of used tiles and the frontier
colours (a first-moment estimate of the number of completions, 33 ns per
evaluation) tilts toward the planted prefix by about 2 standard deviations at
depths 144 and 160 (48 boards; +1.96 [1.53, 2.42] at 144). This one is
**independently re-checked**. It is what a size-bias model predicts, times 1.5 to
1.8. As a prune at depth 144 on one scan direction it gains 1.02 bits [0.72,
1.27], against a ceiling of 2.35 there. With two directions the gain is -0.04
and with four -1.88. The values printed at depths 96 and 128 rest on too few
effective samples and are not gains. No transfer to the real pieces is claimed.

## What it means for a full solution

The ceiling at 144 is 2.4 bits and the measured gain there is about 1. Against a
gap of 56 to 84 bits, a late filter is a constant factor (2 for 1 bit, 5 for the 2.4-bit
ceiling), and the largest gain priced anywhere is about 5.5 bits of nodes. The tests that
refute nearly all wrong boards act in the thin tail of the tree. A filter that
paid would have to bite before depth 96 (17.1 bits of ceiling) and cost almost
nothing; none was found, and arc consistency on the generated boards leaves
43 percent of depth-96 prefixes standing.

The same table prices one loophole: a coarse stratified colour prior (quadrants)
would be worth 23.6 bits under row scan in a first-moment model. That is derived,
nothing was measured on the real pieces, and it stays closed. See
[approaches priced out](/research/lab/experiments/raphael-anjou/full-solve/approaches-priced-out).

## What was not run

- No filter was built that bites before depth 96 on the real pieces.
- Both tilts and shares come from generated boards with a planted solution; the
  real puzzle's prefix is unknown.
- Pruning at several depths at once was not measured.
- Depths beyond about 190 are not measured; the sampler's population dies there,
  and the profile argues the tail is negligible (10^-4 of the total).

## Reproduce

```
just experiments approach-pricing
```

This reprints the ceiling table in seconds. The census and arc-consistency
numbers reproduce with `just experiments prefix-statistics analyze` and the
`ac_followup.py` script in that folder.
