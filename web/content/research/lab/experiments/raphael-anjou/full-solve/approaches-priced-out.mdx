Can any family of approaches remove the 56 to 73 bits between the row-scan tree
and the budgets of 2^78 and 2^61.5 nodes? The fixed numbers (cost, budgets, gaps) are on the [series hub](/research/lab/experiments/raphael-anjou/full-solve)
and are not re-derived here. The rule for reopening a family: a price of 10 bits
or more and a falsifying experiment under a day. Expected solutions: about 14,702
with the starter clue, about 1 with all five.

## How each family was priced

Each family got a desk study that states a mechanism and prices it in bits
against the row-scan tree, then a second analysis that re-derived the numbers,
re-ran the scripts and corrected what it found. The table carries the largest
gain that survived that second analysis. Results on generated boards are toys,
never Eternity II progress. The gap between a gain and 56 to 73 bits is the
point, so a family is "empty" here only in the sense the last column says.

Three of these rows are weaker than the word "empty" suggests. Search that
learns is unproven in general: the closure covers a store of reasons bolted onto
a row scan, not a clause-learning solver as such, and the measured lead over
propagation alone is flat on two sizes only (see
[learning against propagation](/research/lab/experiments/raphael-anjou/full-solve/learning-against-propagation)).
The lossy-representation bound needs a second reader. The linearisation run was
not independently checked. The 17.4 bits for frame nogoods is information about
the frame, not a saving in nodes: the interior under a frame that passes the
corners is still about 10^13.6 nodes.

Semidefinite relaxations are covered on their own page,
[relaxations on small boards](/research/lab/experiments/raphael-anjou/full-solve/relaxations-on-small-boards).

## Static fill orders on the real pieces

Is some static fill order cheaper than row scan? Eighteen orders were measured
on the official pieces with the five clues (population estimates of the full
tree, five repetitions each; [orders-and-pins](https://github.com/raphael-anjou/eternity2/tree/main/research/experiments/orders-and-pins), part A).

| Result | Orders | Reading |
|---|---|---|
| Does not beat row scan | 15 of 18 | Measured, or bounded below by a Markov bound, above row scan's 10^40.453 |
| Undecided | 3 (three annealed orders) | The estimator's population dies at free depth 38 to 48, while row scan's dies at 191 to 198. Their estimates are lower bounds on the tree, not sizes |

The best line scan is row scan from the bottom; the worst line scan, from the
top right to left, is 1.10 bits above it. The three undecided orders are
undecided in both directions: the data neither show them cheaper nor show them
dearer. Part B of the same folder (what a pinned tile is worth) is on
[worth of a known tile](/research/lab/experiments/raphael-anjou/full-solve/worth-of-a-known-tile).

## Why the ceiling stays low

A check that removes every wrong node from free depth d onward at no cost saves
at most 57.1 bits from depth 52, 29.8 from depth 80, 17.1 from depth 96, 6.8
from depth 128 and 0.5 from depth 158. Real wrong-board tests bite late: arc
consistency refutes 57 to 90 percent of sampled wrong prefixes at depths 96 to
128. That is why the largest gain priced is about 5.5 bits of nodes. See
[late filters](/research/lab/experiments/raphael-anjou/full-solve/late-filters).

## Two audits of the closures

A route audit examined 78 lines of the index of routes tried: 47 closures, of
which 32 sound, 10 weak and bearing on a solve, 5 weak but relevant to records
only. It names three reopenings, none above 1 percent odds of a solve: whether the
convex lift is exact at 6x6, the growth of non-tree solvers against the tree on
the same boards, and learning solvers at 7x7 and on real-piece prefixes with a
propagation-only control. The audit is not independently checked.

An anomaly hunt looked for places where measurement disagrees with its model.
Of 51 candidates, 14 disagree beyond noise at face value; none survives a
painted-board null and a count of comparisons. The three-row staircase (+2.85
bits) is not reproduced at ten times the population (135.2 and 135.7 bits, above
row scan). No gain is shown. This note is not independently checked either.

## What this means for a full solution

The largest surviving gain is about 5.5 bits, against a gap of 56 to 73 bits.
Nothing priced here brings the 16x16 within reach of search. It does not prove that no approach exists:
the weak closures above are the places a reader should push. Current records are
on [records](/research/records) and the frontier on
[open problems](/research/open-problems).

## What would reopen this

1. A published method with a class-level gain. Price it against the index of
   routes and the ceiling table first; a check that only bites after depth 128
   cannot pass.
2. Tiles of the solution become available from outside the search. On painted
   boards the best single extra pin is worth about 10 bits and the median one
   about 6, and a 10^15 run needs more than 10, probably 16 to 24, spread pins.
3. Hardware improves by 15 orders of magnitude or more. Every credible multiplier
   together is about 2.5 orders.
4. A re-check or a run overturns a closure: the convex lift is exact at 6x6; the
   far stratum of frame cores resolves below break-even; a non-tree solver's
   work grows by less than 0.75 of the tree's from 6x6 to 7x7; a learning
   solver's lead over propagation alone rises with size.

## What was not run

Degree-3 linearisation at 5x5 (out of memory), the 6x6 convex-lift reading, learning
solvers at 7x7 and on real-piece prefixes, a second reader for the 2.0-bit sketch
bound, and a certificate that row scan is the best static order at the
bottleneck. Many scripts import tools from experiment folders not packaged
with the evidence or run for minutes to hours and were not re-run for this page;
only the three named in the recipe were.
