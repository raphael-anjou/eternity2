A search that forgets why a branch died pays for it again. These experiments
remember dead ends, or key the search on a few colours so they are recognised
sooner. Each question, baseline and stop line was fixed before any test run.
Each result was independently re-checked; where a README and that re-check
disagreed, the re-check decided and the README was corrected. The price of a
full search and the gap are on the
[series page](/research/lab/experiments/raphael-anjou/full-solve).

## The five at a glance

Boards of 6x6 to 8x8 are generated boards (toys), never Eternity II progress.
The three real-piece experiments use the official 16x16 pieces, with no record
attempt.

| Experiment | Question | Baseline | Stop line | Outcome | Headline number |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| difference-map-bands (generated) | Do exact band projections slow a difference map's growth with size? | width 0, `bb-mrv` | killed if no fall of the slope | **killed** at this cap | 0 of 30 solved at 7x7, every width; `bb-mrv` 23 of 30 |
| cut-keyed-replay (generated) | Does partitioning propagation on a cut line prune because of the cut? | `bb-mrv` nodes, random key | killed if a random key is within 0.1 | **killed** | cut 0.486 of nodes, random 0.419, work 1.21 |
| quota-keying (real) | Is the published colour keying worth 450 to 4,600 times? | default keying | contradicted if gain below 45 | **kept**; claim contradicted | gain 1.31 [0.69, 2.43] |
| five-clue-keying (real) | With five clues, is a one-ring keying a lever? | default, random keys | not a lever if best vs random includes 1 | **killed**, confounded | 0 of 2,240 runs reach legal depth 201 |
| five-clue-quota (real) | Keying or quota curve? | default keying and quota | slack explains if 0 of 12+ vs 8+ openings | **kept**: slack explains it | 0 of 16 against 14 of 16 openings |

"Kept" means the measurement is valid, reproduces exactly and its readings
stand. It does not mean a method works.

## Difference-map bands (killed)

Exact projections onto bands of width k (a dynamic program), k = 0 to 3, were
added to a difference map (an iterative projection method) on 30 generated
boards per size, 3 seeds each, 20,000 iterations per run. At 7x7 and 8x8 no
instance was solved at any width (one run of 90 aside), so no growth exponent
was measured: the kill is of this method at this cap. At 6x6, the only size
with finite medians, the iteration ratio to width 0 has intervals [0.41, 1.36],
[0.43, 1.30] and [0.77, +inf] for widths 1 to 3, all containing 1, and a width
costs 3 to 33 times more per iteration. A larger cap could give finite 7x7
medians at low width, but the 6x6 trend and the censored runs point the wrong
way. Independently re-checked: 90 rows
rerun, identical.

## Cut-keyed replay (killed)

Partitions keyed on the colours of two cut edges were replayed over the exact
trees of `bb-mrv` on 60 generated boards. The cut key keeps 0.486 [0.469,
0.500] of the nodes at 7x7, just under the 0.5 line. A random key with the same
partition count, drawn off the cut line, keeps fewer: 0.419 at 7x7, on every
control seed and on 30 of 30 instances (0.444 against 0.483 at 6x6). The
pruning comes from partitioning in general (every keyed arm keeps 0.42 to
0.49), not from the cut, and every arm costs more propagation work than
`bb-mrv` (1.211 for the cut key). Not tested: a cut key inside a row-scan
solver. Independently re-checked: 6x6 and a sample of 7x7 rows reproduce.

## Quota keying (kept)

The published record engine prunes below a quota of "privileged" colour sides
placed by each cell. An earlier desk study claimed its keying makes reaching
depth 201 450 to 4,600 times cheaper (depth counts cells placed, out of 256). Measured on the real pieces, every node
charged, 10 openings per arm, 1e9 nodes per run, published triple (colours 5,
20, 7 in this repository's numbering) against the port's default:

- **Keying gain: contradicted.** 1.31 [0.69, 2.43] with no clue, 0.74 [0.42,
  1.27] with the centre clue. With five clues the published keying never
  reached depth 201 in 10 runs; the default did 135 times. Scope: 1e9 nodes,
  one attempt, this port (the desk study ran 1e10 with five clues).
- **Legal break path: supported, at a price.** Forbidding illegal break
  placements gives 7.63 [4.82, 13.9] times more legal nodes past depth 201 per
  node, but runs about 23 times slower per node (1.25 arrivals per second
  against 19.2).
- **Staging: dead for the desk study's schedule on this port.** Requiring all
  122 privileged sides by depth 176 cuts deep nodes (depth 230 and beyond) to
  0.051 [0.031, 0.081] of the published schedule's; a third colour by depth 200
  cuts them to 0, which a row scan can barely supply, so that is not a yield
  measurement. Dead if both ratios fall below 3.

Independently re-checked: 19 rows rerun, identical.

## Five-clue keying (killed)

With the five official clues, 680 keyings (one ring colour, two interior
colours, quota scaled to the keying) were screened on 3 openings, and the best
five, the published triple and five random keys confirmed on 20 more. None of
the 2,240 one-ring runs placed a legal node at depth 201 or more; the default
keying placed 9,758 on the 20 confirmation openings. The reading "not a lever"
is a floor effect (best against random is 0 over 0, set to 1), and the default
differed in two things at once, keying and quota slack. The surviving claim is
narrow: no one-ring keying under the scaled quota works. Re-checked: 20 rows
rerun, identical.

## Five-clue quota (kept)

This separated the two. Same five clues, 16 openings, 32 arms. The default
keying, asked for 147 of its 150 sides by cell 160 instead of 119, never passes
depth 35: 0 of 16 openings reach depth 201 (12 under its own quota). One-ring
keyings asked for the same 79% share of their sides have 14 of 16 (published
triple) and 12 to 13 (keying 1, 7, 9) positive openings, against 2 and 1 under
their earlier 97.5% quota. Legal nodes past depth 201 per second for the
published triple rise from 0.19 to 10.0. **Quota slack, not the colour set,
explained the previous experiment.**

Not shown: at matched slack the default is still ahead on the point estimate
(one-ring keyings at 0.61 [0.30, 1.53] and 0.74 [0.29, 2.18] of its rate per
second), with intervals including 1, so keying is not shown to matter on 16
openings. Which segment of the curve binds is not localised. The port's
top-row orientation fix is free (0.98 [0.95, 1.04]). Deepest legal depth of any
run: 220. Independently re-checked: 8 rows rerun, identical.

## What this means for a full solution

None of the five makes a full solve cheaper. Taking base-2 logarithms of the
ratios above (derived), and pretending every effect transferred and added,
which they do not:

| Effect | Factor | Bits |
|---|---|---|
| cut key, generated boards, nodes only (work rises) | 1 / 0.486 | 1.0 |
| published keying gain, no clue | 1.31 | 0.4 |
| legal break path, per node (a wall-clock loss) | 7.63 | 2.9 |
| quota slack, rate of reaching depth 201 | about 53 | 5.7 |

The row scan with five clues costs 2^134.4 nodes, about 10^15 nodes are
affordable, and the gap is 56 to 73 bits. All four together are about 10 bits,
leaving at least 46 of the 56. The kept result is a rate of reaching a depth,
not a smaller tree, and a deeper run is not a higher-scoring board (see
[the records](/research/records)). See also
[late filters](/research/lab/experiments/raphael-anjou/full-solve/late-filters).

## What was not run

No other quota curve shape, no budget above 1e9 nodes per run, no restarts, no
border interleaving, no cut key inside a row-scan solver, no other band
projection.

## Reproduce

The kept experiment:

```
just experiments five-clue-quota test 4 local
```

The other four:

```
just experiments difference-map-bands
just experiments cut-keyed-replay test 7 12
just experiments quota-keying main 12
just experiments five-clue-keying screen 12
```
