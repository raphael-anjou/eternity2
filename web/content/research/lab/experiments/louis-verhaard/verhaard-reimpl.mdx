</Callout>

Verhaard's own eii is a source-less Windows binary, and it
[has not been run here](/research/lab/experiments/louis-verhaard/eii). To put his
approach on the benchmark it had to be rebuilt, and this is that rebuild: the
same engine that appears as `verhaard` on the
[grid](/research/lab/experiments/single-core-benchmark).

## What it reimplements

A depth-first backtracker in the shape the
[eii page](/research/lab/experiments/louis-verhaard/eii) describes:

- **Comb fill order.** The border ring first (corners, then edges), then the
  interior filled mostly in horizontal rows with the remaining rows vertical.
- **Depth-gated edge slipping.** A schedule that allows a growing number of
  cumulative interior-edge mismatches past depth 193 (up to 12 by depth 240);
  border edges never slip.
- **A depth quota over good groups.** At set depths the board must already hold
  a minimum count of "good" pieces, drawn from one of 60 groups of 253 pieces
  chosen at random per restart.
- **A restart portfolio**, each restart with a shuffled opening and a node cap.

The quota table and the 60 good groups are byte-exact from `eii.exe` (its
`.rdata` and `.data` sections). The slip schedule, the single most important
lever, is not stored as a table in the v1.0 binary; it is the tuned schedule
from Verhaard's published posts. The
[engine source](https://github.com/raphael-anjou/eternity2/tree/main/research/experiments/single-core-benchmark/engine/crates/bench-audit/src/bin)
(`verhaard_faithful_v2.rs`) records which constant came from where.

Calling this "running Verhaard" is a stretch worth naming: it is a reading of his
method, not his code, and it has not been validated against his original's score
distribution.

## What it does on the benchmark variants

Ten variants of the official puzzle, each pinning all five official clues plus
three corners; one thread, 60 seconds, fixed seed:

- **Mean 440.8 / 480**, best **451** (variants 3 and 7), worst 437; every board
  fully placed (256 of 256) with all five clues respected.
- About **24 million** search-nodes a second.

Every one of these boards is committed in the engine's
[backing directory](https://github.com/raphael-anjou/eternity2/tree/main/research/experiments/single-core-benchmark),
and the grid reruns them. The variants are more constrained than the plain
five-clue puzzle (they add three pinned corners), so this is not a score on an
easier instance. Within the budget each run stops improving well before the 60
seconds are up; that is a plateau under this budget and seed, not a proof of a
local optimum. Against our Blackwood-style engine on the same variants the order
is not separated at n = 10 (see the
[grid](/research/lab/experiments/single-core-benchmark#reading-the-table)).

The three community engines studied in this section are not measured on the
same axis, so the comparison needs care.
[Blackwood's published program](/research/lab/experiments/joshua-blackwood/solver),
with a crude five-clue pin bolted on, reached a placement *depth* of about 45,
not a matched-edge score; our clue-aware Blackwood-style reimplementation
averages 436.4 edges on these variants.
[McGavin's](/research/lab/experiments/peter-mcgavin/backtracker) figure is
also a placement depth (about 204 of 256 pieces reached). Neither depth can be
read on the same scale as the matched-edge scores here.
