The [single-core benchmark](/research/lab/experiments/single-core-benchmark)
leaderboard shows the methods that compete on score. This page shows the rest:
a dozen presets of one constraint-satisfaction engine, plus the naive DFS in its
weaker visit order. They are not a dozen solvers. They are one arc-consistency
core wearing different heads, kept so the exact cost of each classic technique
can be read off a number instead of argued about.

## What each knob buys

The presets vary two things: the order the engine assigns cells, and how hard it
propagates before committing. Where two presets differ in exactly one knob and
that knob is live, the score gap reads as its value at this budget, on these ten
instances, with one seed.

- **Least-constraining-value ordering helps.** `anjou-gacolor_ac3_lcv` reaches a
  mean of 114 against the plain `anjou-gacolor_ac3` at 76, and value order is
  the only difference between them: choosing the value that rules out the fewest
  neighbours is worth about 38 points here.
- **Three groups of presets resolve to the same search.** `anjou-border_first_lcv`
  and `anjou-rare_color_first`; `anjou-gacolor_ac3`, `anjou-gacolor_ac3_ns1` and
  `anjou-verhaard_preferred`; `anjou-joe_depth150` and `anjou-joe_depth150_bp`:
  each group emitted the same board on all ten variants
  ([`contender_stats.py`](https://github.com/raphael-anjou/eternity2/tree/main/research/experiments/single-core-benchmark/scripts)
  checks it). The engine code accounts for three of the four knobs: the
  rare-colour cell order is still a placeholder that sorts cells exactly like
  border-first MRV, and the preferred-piece and BP-marginal value orders only act
  when the caller supplies a preferred-piece list or BP marginals, which the grid
  does not. NS-1 multiset equality is wired in but did not change the search on
  any of these runs. No value can be read off these knobs here: they were not
  exercised, which is not the same as inert.
- **Border-first MRV with light propagation is the strongest preset.** Despite
  its name, `anjou-border_first_lcv` uses insertion value order, not LCV; it
  averages about 183. Border-first alone does not explain that.
  `anjou-border_first_full`, the same cell order plus parity and island
  propagators, averages 124 at 11 thousand nodes per second instead of 279
  thousand, and `anjou-border_first_random`, border-first with random tie-breaks
  in place of MRV, averages 39. The MRV tie-break and the absence of costly
  propagators carry the score. It is still less than half a contender's score.

## Why none of them competes

The border-first and depth-150 presets are bimodal. `anjou-border_first_lcv`
reaches 341 to 349 on four corner arrangements and collapses to 54 to 68 on
five; `anjou-joe_depth150` sits at 243 to 249 on six and 53 to 55 on four. On
the hostile arrangements the search stays in a bad region it cannot leave inside
60 seconds, and the mean sits low because those variants drag it down. The
`anjou-gacolor_ac3` family is not bimodal: it spreads from 56 to 160 with no
clear second mode, slowed by full arc-consistency at every node. No ordering knob
tested here fixes the collapse. The backtrackers on the leaderboard never drop
below 431 on any variant, which is why a preset at 183 sits on a different page
from the leaderboard's contenders, which top out at 451.

## The naive DFS is here too, for one reason

Naive depth-first search with every break allowed is not a CSP preset, but its
visit order belongs to the same lesson. In row-major order
(`anjou-naive_rowmajor`, on the leaderboard as the baseline) it reaches 365; the
same DFS in spiral visit order (`anjou-naive_spiral`) collapses to a mean of 78.
The visit-order choice alone costs the naive search nearly 290 points, the same
kind of ordering sensitivity the CSP presets show, at a larger scale.

## Method and reproducibility

These presets ran in the same grid as the leaderboard: ten official-puzzle
variants, each with three pinned corner cells, single core, 60 seconds, fixed
seed, one run per variant. Every board is re-scored by the one canonical
matched-edge scorer, never the engine's self-report. The engine, the ten
variants, the committed per-run results and the scripts live under the
experiment's
[backing directory](https://github.com/raphael-anjou/eternity2/tree/main/research/experiments/single-core-benchmark),
and `just experiments single-core-benchmark` reruns the whole grid, contenders
and presets together.
